# Biz-kit ethos

AAA describes what a business wants for itself. TTT describes what businesses
need from one another and from Biz-kit when they work together.

## AAA — Wants

- **Authority.** Each business should be able to establish and exercise its
  authority to do business where its partners operate. It decides who may act
  on its behalf, and every commitment can be traced to the authority behind it.
- **Autonomy.** Each business chooses which markets to enter and how to
  operate in those markets. It decides whom to work with, what terms to
  accept, and which decisions to delegate to people or software.
- **Access.** Businesses can reach partners where those partners operate,
  participate in the markets where they want to buy or sell, and use shared
  services on clear terms. Their authorized representatives can get the
  tools and records they need.

## TTT — Needs

- **Truth.** The record shows what the evidence establishes, which claims are
  still open, and how errors were corrected.
- **Transparency.** Each party can see and understand the terms, costs, and
  decisions that affect it, with confidential information protected.
- **Trust.** Businesses earn trust by keeping commitments, applying agreed
  rules consistently, and resolving disputes.

## Example — Cooperative group purchase

A cooperative group purchase can connect member businesses with a supplier in
another market. Each member confirms its authority to trade with the supplier
and sees its share of the supplier price and service costs before choosing
whether to participate. An authorized representative approves the commitment
for that business. The business decides how to receive and verify the delivery
under the agreed terms. It can reconcile the charge against the supplier
invoice and delivery record and use those records to raise a discrepancy.
