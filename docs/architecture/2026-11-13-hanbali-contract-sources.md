# Hanbali contract source register

Retrieval date: **2026-11-13**. This register accompanies the
[Biz-kit Hanbali contract specification](2026-11-13-hanbali-commercial-contract-taxonomy.md).
`H` identifiers cover classical Hanbali material or a
documented intra-school disagreement; `M` identifiers cover modern collective
decisions. Links to classical books reproduce the named work; the
hosting website is not the author of that work.

**Evidence is not adoption or acceptance.** A citation establishes the scope of a
documented opinion. It does not select that opinion for a business, establish the
facts of a transaction, approve its civil-law treatment, or demonstrate that a
runtime enforces it. A modern collective decision is not automatically the
classical Hanbali position. Locations follow the linked editions.

## H01 — Ordinary sale, tawliya and murabaha

- **Sources:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*,
  [sale by musawama, murabaha or tawliya](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3018/?idfrom=&idto=&start=);
  4:130, §3049,
  [murabaha](https://www.islamweb.org/ar/library/content/15/2468/مسألة-بيع-المرابحة);
  4:132–133, §3053,
  [disclosure of the original purchase](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/2470/فصل-اشترى-شيئا-بثمن-مؤجل-لم-يجز-بيعه-مرابحة);
  4:134, §3059,
  [tawliya](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/2474/فصل-بيع-التولية).
- **Supports:** Ordinary negotiated-price sale (musawama) is distinguished from
  the cost-based modes. Murabaha states the known acquisition cost and known profit;
  tawliya resells at the original price. Relevant facts about a deferred or
  related-party acquisition must be disclosed for the cost-based representation.
- **Limit:** Recognition of these modes does not establish every condition of a
  particular sale. The passages do not approve a modern promise-and-agency bundle,
  prove possession, or make every overhead allocation part of the original cost.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H02 — Remunerated agency

- **Source:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Agency, §3745,
  [agency with and without remuneration](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3114/فصل-التوكيل-بجعل-وغير-جعل).
- **Supports:** Agency may be paid or unpaid. Entitlement to remuneration follows
  the agreed work and conditions; an express requirement to collect and deliver
  sale proceeds changes when the fee is earned.
- **Limit:** Agency authority, the principal's property, and the agent's own
  trading must remain distinguishable. This is not authority to charge for a loan.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H03 — Ijara

- **Sources:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Leases,
  [permissibility and uses of ijara](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3516/كتاب-الإجارات),
  and [known duration and compensation](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3518/مسألة-إذا-وقعت-الإجارة-على-مدة-معلومة-بأجرة-معلومة).
- **Supports:** Leasing usable assets and hiring lawful work are recognized
  transactions; the discussion includes premises, transport and crafts, and
  specifying the leased use by known duration or work with known compensation.
- **Limit:** General recognition does not settle every allocation of maintenance,
  ownership risk or later purchase rights. Modern lease-to-own requirements are
  recorded separately in M04.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H04 — Ju'ala

- **Sources:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Lost Property, §4530,
  [known compensation and lawful work](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3871/فصل-الجعالة-تساوي-الإجارة-في-اعتبار-العلم-بالعوض);
  al-Buhuti, *Kashshaf al-Qina'*, Book of Ju'ala,
  [uncertainty in the amount of work](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/16/5690/باب-الجعالة).
- **Supports:** A reward may be attached to a lawful result for known
  compensation. Ju'ala can tolerate uncertainty in the work that ijara cannot,
  within its own rules of termination and entitlement.
- **Limit:** Not every per-job service is ju'ala. These passages do not prove that
  a software completion signal establishes actual performance or resolve every
  disputed form of percentage compensation.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H05 — Partnership families

- **Source:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Partnership,
  [opening definition](https://www.islamweb.net/amp/ar/library/content/15/3003/كتاب-الشركة).
- **Supports:** Distinguishes shared ownership from contractual partnership and
  identifies inan, abdan, wujuh, mudaraba and mufawada as contractual families.
- **Limit:** The list is a taxonomy, not approval of every arrangement bearing
  one of these names. Corporate membership and ownership require their own terms.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h06"></a>

## H06 — Partnership profit allocation

- **Source:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Partnership, §3649,
  [profit according to the agreed allocation](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3028/مسألة-الربح-على-ما-اصطلحا-عليه-في-جميع-أقسام-الشركة).
- **Supports:** Known shares of actual profit can reflect permitted differences
  in work as well as capital. The discussion restricts an excess allocation to a
  wholly passive contributor in the mixed capital-and-work example.
- **Limit:** This is not permission for every conceivable ratio, a fixed return
  on principal, or allocation of nonexistent profit. Apply the rules of the
  particular partnership and contribution roles.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h07"></a>

## H07 — Ordinary partnership loss

- **Source:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Partnership,
  [loss according to contributed capital](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3035/مسألة-الخسران-في-الشركة-على-قدر-المال).
- **Supports:** Ordinary investment losses follow the capital contributions,
  rather than a freely chosen profit ratio.
- **Limit:** This does not determine separately established liability for
  misconduct or unauthorized conduct. Wujuh requires the ownership-and-liability
  treatment described in H11, not an invented cash-capital ratio.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h08"></a>

## H08 — Mudarib as entrusted manager

- **Source:** al-Buhuti, *Kashshaf al-Qina'*, 3:523,
  [the manager as trustee of mudaraba property](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/16/4561/فصل-العامل-أمين-في-مال-المضاربة).
- **Supports:** The mudarib holds investment property in trust and does not
  automatically guarantee an ordinary investment loss; fault and breach require
  their own liability analysis.
- **Limit:** An adverse commercial result is not itself proof of negligence.
  Conversely, the trustee classification does not excuse established misuse or
  conduct outside the mandate.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h09"></a>

## H09 — Abdan work partnership

- **Sources:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Partnership, §3615,
  [work partnership](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3004/مسألة-شركة-الأبدان),
  and §§3618–3619,
  [allocation of earnings](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3006/فصل-الربح-في-شركة-الأبدان).
- **Supports:** Partners may undertake work and share its earnings under agreed
  terms; carriers and crafts are discussed, including unequal earnings shares.
- **Limit:** Different-craft partnerships have intra-school disagreement. Joint
  work, mere referrals, employment and separately owned asset rentals must not be
  collapsed into one classification.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h10"></a>

## H10 — Joint transport work versus pooled asset rents

- **Source:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Partnership, §3620,
  [separately owned transport animals and shared carriage obligations](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3007/فصل-اشترك-رجلان-لكل-واحد-منهما-دابة-على-أن-يؤجراهما-فما-رزقهما-الله-من-شيء-فهو-بينهما).
- **Supports:** Jointly undertaking carriage for others is distinguished from
  merely pooling rental receipts from separately owned assets.
- **Limit:** Calling a fleet a work partnership does not change who owns each
  asset or who earned its rent. Modern dispatch arrangements require a defined
  work obligation, or a separate lease/agency/ownership analysis.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h11"></a>

## H11 — Wujuh credit-based trading partnership

- **Source:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Partnership,
  [the passage on wujuh](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3012/مسألة-اشترك-بدنان-بمال-أحدهما-أو-بدنان-بمال-غيرهما).
- **Supports:** Partners may purchase goods on supplier credit based on their
  standing, with defined ownership and responsibility, then trade those goods.
  Ordinary losses follow ownership in what was acquired.
- **Limit:** Absence of initial cash capital does not mean absence of real goods,
  debts or liability. The passage does not authorize unbacked platform credits.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h12"></a>

## H12 — Scoped mufawada

- **Source:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Partnership,
  [two forms of mufawada](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3027/فصل-شركة-المفاوضة).
- **Supports:** Valid partnership forms may be combined within their permissible
  scope. The text rejects extending the arrangement indiscriminately to unrelated
  inheritances, windfalls and liabilities.
- **Limit:** A network-wide label does not merge every member's assets,
  obligations or authority. Each constituent relationship must remain valid.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H13 — Capital contributed in kind

- **Source:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Partnership, §3628,
  [inan and the form of its capital](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3014/فصل-شركة-العنان).
- **Supports:** The account presents cash capital as the apparent school
  position and records another report permitting goods valued when contributed.
- **Limit:** Inventory or equipment subscriptions require an explicit opinion
  selection, valuation and transfer of ownership. A valuation alone does not
  establish that the asset was contributed or that any selected profile allows it.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h14"></a>

## H14 — Existing debt and mudaraba capital

- **Sources:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Partnership,
  [instructing a debtor to invest the debt as mudaraba](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3084/مسألة-قال-لمن-عليه-دين-ضارب-بالدين-الذي-عليك),
  and [collecting a third-party debt before investing](https://www.islamweb.net/amp/ar/library/content/15/3085/فصل-قال-لرجل-اقبض-المال-الذي-على-فلان-واعمل-به-مضاربة).
- **Supports:** A receivable is not made into mudaraba capital merely by telling
  the debtor to trade with the amount owed. Collection and a valid subsequent
  contribution must be distinguished from relabelling the debt; the separate
  passage permits an authorized agent to collect a third-party debt and invest
  the received property.
- **Limit:** This does not prohibit every separately justified debt settlement
  and later investment. It does prohibit treating an accounting label as evidence
  that the required intervening act occurred.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H15 — Stipulated benefits from qard

- **Sources:** al-Buhuti, *Kashshaf al-Qina'*, Book of Loans,
  [stipulated loan benefits](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/16/3772/باب-القرض);
  Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, Book of Loans,
  [conditions attached to a loan](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/4200/360?lang=ar).
- **Supports:** A stipulated increase or qualifying benefit for the lender is
  prohibited; the discussion includes tying a loan to a sale, rent or another
  loan.
- **Limit:** A flat fee, separate document or membership label does not establish
  independence from lending. This is not a claim that every incidental mutual
  convenience is prohibited; actual expense recovery is considered in M02.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H16 — Qard repayment term

- **Sources:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, 4:208–209,
  [the lender's present claim](https://www.islamweb.org/ar/library/content/15/2656/فصل-للمقرض-المطالبة-ببدله-في-الحال);
  al-Buhuti, *Kashshaf al-Qina'*, 3:316,
  [the traditional rule and Ibn Taymiyya's selection](https://islamweb.org/ar/library/content/16/3768/باب-القرض).
- **Supports:** The traditional account treats qard as presently claimable while
  encouraging fulfillment of a promised term. Al-Buhuti also records Ibn
  Taymiyya's position that the agreed term is binding.
- **Limit:** A predictable fixed-term member loan requires an explicit opinion
  selection. A generic Hanbali label does not resolve this disagreement.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h17"></a>

## H17 — Matching reciprocal debts and muqassa

- **Sources:** al-Buhuti, *Sharh Muntaha al-Iradat*, 3:15,
  [general matching-debt rule within the cited chapter](https://www.islamweb.net/amp/ar/library/content/21/3114/فصل-تزوج-عبد-بإذن-سيده);
  Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*,
  [different debt genera](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/7677/?idfrom=&idto=&start=).
- **Supports:** Matched reciprocal debts share genus, quality and maturity; they
  may both be due or share a deferred date. Equal amounts cancel and excess
  remains. The second passage rejects cross-genus setoff in the ordinary case,
  even with consent; H25 records a distinct juristic analysis.
- **Limit:** This is not blanket authorization for every currency conversion or
  multilateral cycle. Matching ledger amounts do not establish creditor identity,
  eligibility or effective discharge. See M07 for a distinct currency rule.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h18"></a>

## H18 — Hawala and guarantee

- **Source:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*,
  [Book of Hawala and Daman](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/المغني_-_كتاب_الحوالة_والضمان),
  especially the conditions of hawala and its distinction from agency.
- **Supports:** Hawala transfers a qualifying claim to another obligor under
  conditions concerning established debts, known amounts, matching type,
  quality and maturity. Guarantee is a separately treated obligation.
- **Limit:** Missing underlying debts can make an arrangement agency or a loan
  instead of the described hawala. Neither a chain of invoices nor a modern
  remittance label establishes this classical classification. Guarantee fees are
  separately addressed in M06.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h19"></a>

## H19 — Voluntary release and settlement

- **Source:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, 4:312–313,
  [settlement after acknowledgment of a right](https://gcp.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/2891/مسألة-اعترف-بحق-فصالح-على-بعضه).
- **Supports:** A creditor may voluntarily release part of an acknowledged
  entitlement; coercing a reduction through withholding an admitted right is
  distinguished from genuine consent. A substitute countervalue remains subject
  to its exchange rules.
- **Limit:** An agent cannot infer a waiver merely from silence or accounting
  convenience. This passage does not approve every prearranged debt discount.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H20 — Agricultural output partnerships

- **Sources:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*,
  [Book of Musaqa](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3466/كتاب-المساقاة),
  [a known undivided fruit share](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3470/فصل-المساقاة-لا-تصح-إلا-على-جزء-معلوم-من-الثمرة-مشاع),
  and [muzara'a for part of the crop](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3499/مسألة-المزارعة-ببعض-ما-يخرج-من-الأرض).
- **Supports:** Orchard care and crop cultivation may be organized around known
  proportional shares of their actual output.
- **Limit:** A share of output is not a guaranteed fixed yield or cash return.
  General acceptance does not settle every seed, input, land and labor allocation.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H21 — Classical treatment of manufacturing orders

- **Sources:** al-Mardawi, *al-Insaf*, Book of Sales,
  [note on manufacturing outside salam](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/26/2292/?idfrom=&idto=&start=);
  Muhammad ibn Muflih, *al-Furu'*, 4:23–25,
  [sale by description and the manufacturing discussion](https://www.islamweb.net/amp/ar/library/content/28/2258/مسألة-بيع-الموصوف-غير-المعين).
- **Supports:** The texts report al-Qadi and his companions rejecting the
  described manufacture-to-order sale outside salam, and discuss related
  partly-manufactured goods and combined-contract issues.
- **Limit:** This is direct evidence that the modern independent form in M05
  cannot simply be labelled an identical classical Hanbali rule. It does not
  establish unanimity on every manufacturing or labor arrangement.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H22 — Urbun sale and precontract holding payment

- **Sources:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, 4:160–161, §3128,
  [urbun](https://www.islamweb.com/ar/library/content/15/2535/فصل-في-بيع-العربون);
  al-Mardawi, *al-Insaf*, 4:358,
  [predominant opinion and time-limit disagreement](https://www.islamweb.com/ar/library/content/26/2332/باب-الشروط-في-البيع);
  al-Buhuti, *Kashshaf al-Qina'*, 3:195,
  [sale, lease and precontract payment](https://www.islamweb.com/ar/library/content/16/3337/فصل-ما-يحرم-اشتراطه-في-البيع).
- **Supports:** The predominant Hanbali position permits an actual sale or lease
  with earnest money credited on completion and retained on the buyer's agreed
  withdrawal. The texts distinguish a mere precontract holding payment, which is
  recoverable if no contract follows. Al-Insaf records both time-limited and
  unspecified-duration formulations, alongside dissent on validity.
- **Limit:** A demand signal or promise is not automatically an urbun sale. A
  finite deadline is required by M08 and has classical precedent, but is not an
  undisputed classical requirement. Urbun does not authorize trading an option.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H23 — Waqf corpus, administration and cash distinction

- **Sources:** al-Buhuti, *Kashshaf al-Qina'*, 4:244–245,
  [property consumed through use, including lending cash](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/16/5959/كتاب-الوقف),
  and 4:266–267,
  [founder's conditions and preservation of the endowment](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/16/6001/فصل-يرجع-إلى-شرط-الواقف-في-الناظر-فيه).
- **Supports:** Waqf preserves a corpus and directs its benefits under its valid
  conditions. The cited treatment rejects a cash endowment used through lending
  because the particular corpus is consumed, while separately addressing upkeep
  and preservation of endowed property.
- **Limit:** A revolving cash pool is not automatically the same as a classical
  durable-asset waqf. An alternative cash-waqf profile requires its own adopted
  authority. Ordinary member equity is not converted into waqf by bookkeeping.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

## H24 — Salam prepayment

- **Sources:** al-Khiraqi, *Mukhtasar al-Khiraqi*, Book of Salam, §§725–726,
  [specification, delivery and payment conditions](https://fiqh.islamicportal360.com/ar/book/9008463/53/);
  Ibn Muflih, *al-Mubdi' fi Sharh al-Muqni'*, 4:194–196,
  [receipt of salam capital](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/96/1341/السادس-أن-يقبض-رأس-المال-في-مجلس-العقد).
- **Supports:** Salam requires sufficiently specified goods, known quantity and
  delivery term, and receipt of the capital before separation. The cited rule
  rejects simply designating an existing debt as salam capital and distinguishes
  receipt of all capital from receipt of only part.
- **Limit:** Ordinary accounting netting is not proof of the required prepayment.
  This group does not approve every form of constructive possession or a resale
  of the salam goods before receipt. H25 records a distinct contrary analysis of
  some debt-substitution cases; it is not silently adopted here.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h25"></a>

## H25 — Distinctions within debt-for-debt analysis

- **Source:** Ibn al-Qayyim, *I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in*, 2:293–294,
  [hawala and the different debt-exchange forms](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/34/122/?idfrom=&idto=&start=),
  reporting and endorsing relevant reasoning of Ibn Taymiyya.
- **Supports:** The text distinguishes creating two deferred obligations from
  extinguishing reciprocal debts and other substitutions. It expressly disputes
  claimed consensus against some forms, including the described debt-to-salam
  substitution, and treats hawala as fulfillment of rights.
- **Limit:** This disagreement prevents a blanket historical claim that every
  debt-for-debt arrangement is forbidden. It does not itself approve every
  netting cycle, currency exchange or implementation, or override the selected
  H24 profile without an explicit adoption decision.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h26"></a>

## H26 — Rahn collateral

- **Source:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*,
  [Book of Rahn](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/المغني_-_كتاب_الرهن),
  the discussion of a sale conditioned on known collateral or a guarantor and
  the discussion of collateral for the same loan debt.
- **Supports:** An identified pledge may secure an eligible sale-price or loan
  obligation; security is distinct from the underlying exchange or loan.
- **Limit:** A pledge does not itself transfer ownership to the creditor or
  convert collateral into sale proceeds. Realization, debt satisfaction and the
  owner's remaining rights require their own valid acts and evidence.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="h27"></a>

## H27 — Mudaraba cannot require trading only without profit

- **Sources:** Ibn Qudama, *al-Mughni*, conditions in mudaraba, §3705,
  [conditions concerning sale price and profit](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/15/3079/?idfrom=&idto=&start=),
  and [Book of Partnership](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/المغني_-_كتاب_الشركة),
  subsection dividing defective conditions into three categories.
- **Supports:** Requiring sales only at cost or below defeats the intended
  profit of mudaraba and is a defective condition. The text distinguishes
  unknown-profit conditions from other defects and records differing consequences
  for the contract.
- **Limit:** This is not a prohibition of every occasional sale at cost or loss,
  nor a uniform rule that every defective condition invalidates the contract.
  At-cost co-op services and a profit-seeking investment mandate need distinct
  economic purposes and terms.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="m01"></a>

## M01 — Installment sale

- **Sources:** International Islamic Fiqh Academy (IIFA),
  [Resolution 51 (2/6), 1990](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/33292.html), and
  [Resolution 64 (2/7), 1992](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/32442.html).
- **Supports:** A deferred sale price may exceed the cash price, but the contract
  must select one price and payment basis. The debt may not increase for late
  payment. Security may be taken for the price. Resolution 64 distinguishes a
  bilateral, non-prearranged early-payment reduction from third-party discounting.
- **Limit:** These are modern collective decisions, not proof that every
  mentioned detail is the classical Hanbali position. They do not turn a loan
  surcharge into a sale or approve secondary trading of receivables.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="m02"></a>

## M02 — Loan expenses and acquisition before sale or lease

- **Source:** IIFA,
  [Resolution 13 (1/3), 1986](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/32245.html), sections on
  loan service fees, lease transactions and future installment sales.
- **Supports:** Recovery of actual loan-service expenses is permitted; amounts
  beyond those expenses are prohibited as riba. Separately, the sale and lease
  provisions require actual acquisition and receipt of the equipment before
  concluding the relevant separate contract; an agency or promise is distinct.
- **Limit:** The decision supplies no safe harbor for a fixed membership fee,
  general profit margin or principal/time-based charge. Expense allocation and
  linkage between lending and other fees require examination. Its sale/lease
  provisions do not authorize profitable loan charges.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="m03"></a>

## M03 — Diminishing partnership

- **Source:** IIFA,
  [Resolution 136 (2/15), 2004](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/33542.html).
- **Supports:** The approved structure retains actual partnership risk,
  capital-proportional loss, distinct share sales and ownership-linked costs. It
  allows an appropriate purchase undertaking and use of a partner's share by
  lease, without a par-value undertaking that guarantees investment principal.
- **Limit:** This is contemporary collective approval of a specified composite,
  not the automatic effect of membership installments or a guarantee that equity
  can always be redeemed at its original price.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="m04"></a>

## M04 — Lease ending in ownership

- **Source:** IIFA,
  [Resolution 110 (4/12), 2000](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/32590.html).
- **Supports:** A genuine lease must retain its own rules and ownership risk;
  owner-side maintenance is distinguished from operating expenses. Permitted
  structures separate the later title transfer or use an appropriately framed
  promise, rather than automatically treating rent as a completed sale.
- **Limit:** Approval is form-specific. It does not validate every lease-to-own
  product, simultaneous conflicting contracts, or wholesale transfer of the
  lessor's ownership risk to the lessee.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="m05"></a>

## M05 — Modern independent istisna

- **Sources:** IIFA, Resolution 65, 1992,
  [English](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/33356.html) and
  [Arabic](https://iifa-aifi.org/ar/1852.html).
- **Supports:** A manufacturing contract can bind the parties with specified
  goods and a specified deadline; payment may be deferred or scheduled in
  installments. The resolution permits a performance penalty subject to its
  stated exceptions.
- **Limit:** This is modern collective approval of the specified structure, not
  evidence of an identical classical Hanbali contract. A manufacturing-performance
  penalty is not permission to increase a monetary debt for late payment. The
  Arabic text supplies the deadline requirement truncated in the English webpage.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="m06"></a>

## M06 — Guarantee administration costs

- **Source:** IIFA,
  [Resolution 12 (12/2), 1985](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/33212.html).
- **Supports:** The guarantee itself is not a remunerated risk-bearing service
  under this decision. Actual administrative expenses for issuing a guarantee
  may be recovered within the stated cost conditions, including relevant work
  associated with coverage.
- **Limit:** A guarantee premium linked to amount or duration is not justified
  merely by calling it administration. A guarantee, collateral and paid agency
  remain distinct relationships.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="m07"></a>

## M07 — Settlement in a different currency

- **Source:** IIFA,
  [Resolution 75 (6/8), 1993](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/32475.html), second provision.
- **Supports:** Creditor and debtor may agree on the settlement day to discharge
  a debt in another currency at that day's rate, with no remainder of the amount
  being exchanged and with the required possession rules observed.
- **Limit:** This does not authorize a pre-fixed future exchange rate or every
  multilateral cross-currency netting arrangement. Arithmetic conversion and a
  valid spot settlement are different requirements.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="m08"></a>

## M08 — Modern urbun conditions

- **Sources:** IIFA,
  [Resolution 72 (8/3), 1993, Arabic](https://iifa-aifi.org/ar/1955.html), and
  [Resolution 238 (24/9), 2019, Arabic](https://iifa-aifi.org/ar/5188.html).
- **Supports:** Urbun applies to an actual sale or lease with a finite waiting
  period. It is credited toward the price or retained on the buyer's withdrawal.
  Resolution 72 excludes salam, currency exchange and the promise stage of
  purchase-order murabaha. Resolution 238 requires retention of the underlying
  asset through settlement and prohibits trading the urbun right.
- **Limit:** A promise-stage seriousness deposit securing actual loss is a
  different arrangement. These resolutions do not permit blanket forfeiture of
  demand deposits or a market in separately sold option rights.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.

<a id="m09"></a>

## M09 — Cooperative insurance / takaful

- **Sources:** IIFA,
  [Resolution 9 (9/2), 1985](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/1596.html), and
  [Resolution 200 (6/21), 2013](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/33068.html).
- **Supports:** The resolutions distinguish cooperative risk sharing from the
  specified commercial insurance form. Resolution 200 describes cooperative
  insurance as a new contract and sets out participant-fund and management
  relationships.
- **Limit:** A co-op name or membership contribution does not by itself create
  an approved takaful arrangement. Risk-pool assets, participant rights, manager
  remuneration and investment activities require their own terms. This modern
  collective framework is not an unchanged classical Hanbali nominate contract.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-13.
