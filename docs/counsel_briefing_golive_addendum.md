```

       what each assumes has cleared, and two questions the memo doesn't yet name

```

**How to read this.** The go-live checklists (`docs/outreach/golive_index.md` is
their map) script the network's five debuts: the first brokered load, relay leg,
staged trailer, dock window booking, and shop referral. Each is a gate-check
followed by one ceremony, and each REFUSES to run until its legal items are
"verified, not remembered." This addendum tells you which of your deliverables
each night consumes, and adds two questions (B.4, B.5) that live in no existing
tier. As before: nothing here changes the triage, every checklist is a template
for an event that hasn't happened, and an unanswered item leaves that night
structurally unable to occur.

---

## B.1 — The first routed load [consumes 3.5, 3.6, 3.7; touches 2.1–2.3]

The Face checklist's §0 will not open without: broker authority **granted**, the
bond active, and your written confirmation of the operating posture — plus the
insurance verification chain for the carrier taking the load. Its invoice step
sends only per whatever 2.1–2.3 clearances exist at that date (uncleared =
rendered artifacts, human-sent). *Deliverable when 3.5 engages:* the grant
confirmation practice (what "counsel confirms in writing" should look like so
the checklist item is real, not ceremonial). *Plugs in as:* §0's three boxes.

## B.2 — The first relay leg [consumes the activation checklist's INSURANCE items]

The corridor go-live assumes relay-leg liability at yard boundaries was DECIDED
at activation ("a corridor with unassigned liability is not a corridor; it is a
lawsuit with yards") and that the insurer was notified of staging/relay patterns.
*Deliverable when the first corridor approaches:* the liability-assignment
instrument per leg-boundary, and the insurer-notice form/practice. *Plugs in
as:* corridor activation §4; the go-live merely re-verifies it attached to the
actual first leg.

## B.3 — The first staged trailer [extends the corridor items; one named posture]

Same liability/insurer-disclosure dependencies, per yard. The checklist's
mechanics embed a posture worth your one-line blessing: every possession change
is a dual-signed condition report delivered to the owner on the spot, and
dwell is recorded as events only (no surveillance of her lot). *Deliverable:*
none beyond B.2's, unless the condition-report form itself should carry
language you want.

## B.4 — Bailment at the yard [NEW — no existing tier names it]

A trailer (sometimes loaded) parked on a private lot under a per-drop fee:
whether this creates a bailment (and in whom — the yard owner? the co-op?
neither?) shapes liability, insurance, and what the staging terms should say.
The documents currently rely on the condition-report pair plus the declared
terms, deliberately silent on the legal characterization. *Deliverable when
the first yard listing approaches:* the characterization per our states, and
any sentence the listing terms or condition-report form needs because of it.
*Plugs in as:* the yard listing terms template; possibly one line in the
condition report.

## B.5 — Referral liability at the shop [NEW — no existing tier names it]

The shop flow: we relay a member's need to a shop whose OWN declared
capabilities match it; the member chooses; the shop invoices directly; we take
no fee and touch no money; completions are recorded factually (outcomes, never
stars, never rankings, never "preferred" language). Designed intent: we
transmit his self-declaration, we never certify the shop — and the no-fee
structure keeps us out of any marketplace/agency posture. *Deliverable when
the referral flow approaches:* confirm the negligent-referral exposure is as
low as the structure intends; whether any disclaimer belongs on the referral
message; and confirm the no-fee, no-ranking, self-declared-capabilities
structure creates no undisclosed-agency or platform-regulation surface.
*Plugs in as:* the referral message template, if you want language on it.

## B.6 — The standing pattern (flag only if it worries you)

Every checklist ends in verified refusals — no fee at the shop, no off-book
margin at the Face, no surveillance at the yard, no evidence-free detention
line at the dock. These are commitments made in operational documents rather
than contracts. We believe that's the safe direction (we bind ourselves,
promise nothing to others we don't deliver structurally). *Deliverable:* none
unless the pattern creates an expectation problem we don't see.

---

**Timing summary:** B.1 rides with 3.5–3.7 (the dispatch tool's go-live
quarter, roughly); B.2/B.3/B.4 with the first corridor and yard listings;
B.5 with the shop referral flow — none block the field phase or the build.
The checklists' own rule keeps you in control: a first night whose legal boxes
aren't checked doesn't happen slowly or partially; it doesn't happen.
