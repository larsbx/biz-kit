# Your bays, your prices, your shop — and a calendar that stops starving

*Shop-owner outreach one-pager (Tomás's vignette in 13A is this page's model). Shops
reach this network mostly by member referral — a carrier who trusts this shop is the
natural channel, and the bracket below assumes one. Same rules as every page: zero
printed numbers, structural answers or none, timing brackets picked truthfully.*

---

**[FOUNDER — the channel line: "[CARRIER] has been telling our members about your
shop — this is the network he was talking about." A shop reached without a member's
word behind it has been reached early.]**

## What this is

A carrier-owned cooperative whose members — small fleets and owner-operators — need
exactly what a good shop sells, and whose system routes that need to shops it can
verify are good. **Membership doesn't buy your labor and doesn't want it** — your shop
stays yours, your people stay yours, your prices stay yours. What membership routes to
your door is the thing feast-or-famine shops actually lack: **flow**.

## What arrives (each one mechanical, not aspirational)

- **Referral flow, routed by capability and track record — never by lowest bid.** The
  matching runs on what your shop can do and how your finished jobs actually went
  (signed work events, not star ratings) — **sealed bidding is excluded from this
  system by design**, so flow finding you never means racing to the bottom to keep it.
  Your pricing is between you and the member you're quoting, like it's always been.
- **A queue that fills from your own work orders — so nobody double-books you.** The
  system's picture of your availability is derived from your own records; it doesn't
  ask you to maintain a second calendar, and it can't promise a bay you don't have.
- **Parts through group purchasing, with the spread visible.** When the buying program
  is live, you see what the program pays and what you pay — the difference is audited
  in the open, because hidden spreads are the exact disease this co-op exists to cure.
- **Later, if it ever fits: a bay at a posted rate.** Where a chapter leases space,
  members take bays at the declared card — posted, same for everyone, no negotiation
  theater at move-in.

## The measurement honesty (same deal every facility gets)

Your shop's track record in this network is computed **only from signed job events**,
publishes only above a floor of independent jobs (no single-grudge gotchas), and
**you see exactly the basis we see.** Brigading a shop's score with fake reviews isn't
against the rules here — it's structurally detected, because there are no reviews;
there are signed events from real work.

## Membership, plainly

Shops are a member class — independent business or worker co-op, your structure is
yours (that's explicit in the charter). One member, one vote: your two bays vote the
same as a twenty-truck fleet. Dues buy access and the member rate; they are never a
cut of your work. And your customer relationships are sovereign member data — the
network cannot see, pool, or route around them.

## What we will never do

Route by lowest bid (excluded, above) · buy or borrow your labor (constitutionally
forbidden — the workforce line is a wall in both directions) · demand exclusivity
(your walk-in trade and standing accounts are untouched) · score you on anecdotes ·
promise you volume — flow follows real member demand in your area, and where that
demand isn't live yet, this page says so:

## Where this stands *(pick the true one — delete the other)*

- **[Pre-flow]** Member demand near you isn't routing yet — this conversation is
  early on purpose: your capability listing and your war stories about how work
  *actually* finds a shop help build the routing right. Nothing is owed, nothing is
  promised, and you'll hear it plainly when flow starts.
- **[Post-flow]** Members are running work near you today. I can show you the demand
  in your area live — on the laptop, not on this page.

## The ask

Twenty minutes: what your bays do best, how work finds you today, and the jobs you
turn away that you shouldn't have to. If the network fits, your capabilities list and
the routing does the rest — the buy-in was never going to be this pitch; it'll be the
first month's calendar.
