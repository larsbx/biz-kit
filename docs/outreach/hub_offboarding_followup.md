# Hub / founding-member offboarding follow-up — template (when a founding member departs)

*A founding member departs with THREE files, and this letter is only the third: his
capital has the member redemption letter (whose founding section owns the standing),
his operation has the carrier offboarding letter (export, envelope, in-flight loads —
already written, already carrying its founding bracket), and **this letter closes his
OFFICES — the things only a founder holds.** It never repeats the other two; it names
them. Its mechanical spine: **every role key he holds is rotated and revoked on the
record, governance-signed, with no quorum hole, BEFORE this letter sends.** Its human
spine: he vouched this thing to his community with his name, so the letter settles
the one question unique to a hub — what gets said at the fuel island when people ask.
Series rules; no winback.*

---

**Subject:** Closing the founding offices — the keys, the book, and what gets said
*(alt: "The third file: what only a founder has to hand back, and what no founder can")*

Dear **[first name]**,

Your departure generates three letters, and you hold the other two: your capital is
in the redemption letter, on its schedule, and your operation closed in the carrier
letter, export and all. This one is for what neither covers — the offices, which
only founders accumulate and which don't redeem or export because they were never
yours-the-member's; they were yours-the-officeholder's, held in trust and handed
back on the record.

**The keys, first, because they're done:** every role key registered to you —
**[the list: governance / steward / checkpoint, per the registry]** — was revoked
on the record as of **[date]**, each revocation governance-signed, each successor
key declared before yours went dark. **No gate this co-op runs was ever down to a
missing signer, and the registry proves the handover in order.** The authority you
carried transferred the way it arrived: visibly, signed, on the log — never in a
drawer, never assumed.

**Your sponsorship book:** the members you brought in keep your name in their
provenance permanently — that's history, and history here doesn't edit. **Your
exposure ended with your membership**; their conduct is now theirs alone, and none
of them owes anyone an explanation for staying, least of all you. The vouching had
a ledger, as promised; the ledger shows the vouching closed clean.

**Your books, which became the proof:** the demo every member saw was your
operation, and the record it generated is load-bearing history — sealed, bilateral,
permanent, on exactly the terms as everyone's. Nothing about it gets extracted,
anonymized after the fact, or dressed up; it stays what it always was, the first
true thing this co-op showed anyone. Your export (the carrier letter's) gave you
your copy.

**What gets said, agreed here:** people will ask — at **[the masjid / the fuel
island / the yard]** — because your name is how many of them met this thing. What
we say is one sentence and factual: **"[the agreed sentence — e.g., '[Name] retired
from membership in [month]; his standing is on the record.']"** Nothing more, no
spin, no reading of your reasons — **your story is yours to tell or not tell.** And
the structure you vouched for keeps answering for itself the way you said it would:
anyone who asks whether the thing [name] vouched for was clean can still verify it,
which is the only defense of your name we're entitled to make.

**The constants you wrote:** still governing, unchanged by your departure —
declared rules don't resign with their authors, and the vote that could change them
belongs to current members, under the rule you voted for at the first session. The
capital letter says the rest of that sentence.

**What didn't go right across the founding era, from us first:**

You went first so this letter could be boring, **[first name]** — offices handed
back in order, a book that closed clean, a record that answers for itself. Thank
you for the hour you gave before there was anything to show. The standing that
came from it isn't in any of the three files, because it doesn't leave.

## Usage notes (internal — do not send)

- **Keys BEFORE the letter — the trailers-off rule at governance altitude**: every
  role key of his revoked (RoleKeyRevoked, governance-signed) with successor keys
  declared first, verified from the registry before drafting. A founder's letter
  sent while his governance key still validates gates is not a goodbye; it's an
  open incident. Check quorum coverage per role, per chapter.
- **Three letters, never merged, never repeated**: capital → member redemption
  letter (founding section owns the standing); operations → carrier offboarding
  (export, envelope revocation, in-flight, drivers paragraph). This letter POINTS
  at both and duplicates neither — content moves, never duplicates, applied to
  the letter set itself.
- **The agreed-sentence discipline**: draft the one-line factual statement WITH
  him before he goes; it's the only thing said to anyone who asks, verbatim, by
  everyone who might be asked. No spin in either direction — a departure dressed
  as retirement or a retirement dressed as rift both poison the community channel
  he built. His reasons are his.
- **Sponsorship book verified**: exposure-ends-with-membership confirmed against
  the record (04 §2 scope), provenance untouched. His sponsorees are NOT
  contacted about his departure — the carrier letter's sovereignty rule; they'll
  read the aggregate like every member.
- **The genesis-history paragraph is structural fact**: his streams are ordinary
  bilateral records (07 §5); the demo's provenance stays in the record. Never
  offer to scrub it — append-only isn't negotiable at exit, and he of all
  members knows why.
- **Bad-standing variant**: offices hand back identically — key revocation,
  agreed sentence (factual standing stated), book closure; warmth changes,
  rights and mechanics don't. A founder censured is the system proving the
  founders bound themselves too.
- **Grievance / no-winback / misses-first** — the family's rules; the founding
  era's misses list is MANDATORY and long — an origin story with no potholes
  wasn't looked at.
- **The precedent is constitutional**: the first founder departure proves
  founding standing was never a lever — report to governance as aggregate; the
  ruling organ sees it if any charter constant carried founder-review terms.
