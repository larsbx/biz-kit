# Driver graduation follow-up — template (buy-down completion & title)

*Named precisely: not a redemption — nothing pays out. A **completion**: the last
scheduled payment made, title passing via the separate promise, and — where the
incubator carried him — the sunset firing on schedule. Two gates deep by design:
the instrument counsel-cleared per state (Tier 3.3), then a full term actually run.
This is the letter the driver one-pager's six contrasts were always pointing at —
**each one is checked against the record before drafting, and any that fails leads
the misses.** Sender: the co-op administering the deal; the funders' side closes per
its own declared terms, separately. Series rules; and one absolute: NO second-truck
pitch.*

---

**Subject:** The truck is yours — the whole record, payment by payment
*(alt: "Buy-down complete: the schedule you signed, kept to the end")*

Dear **[first name]**,

Today the title transferred. Before the congratulations — which are real — here's the
part that matters more, because you signed this deal as a skeptic and you're owed the
audit:

**The schedule, kept:** [N] payments over [term], **each one matching the schedule
declared before you signed — the same document, start to finish.** No drift, no
renegotiation, no balloon at the end, no fees that appeared along the way. The
payment-by-payment record against the origination schedule is attached; the two
columns match or this letter doesn't send.

**The six things we said this wasn't — now as record, not promise:**

1. **No interest, by construction:** the total you paid equals the declared schedule —
   no time-value accrual existed anywhere in it, because the contract type cannot
   carry one. Check the arithmetic.
2. **Bound to your name, the whole term:** this truck was yours-in-progress from day
   one — no pool existed for it to churn through; the record shows one name.
3. **Ownership risk stayed on the owner until today:** **[where an event occurred:
   "when [the event] took the truck down [n] days, the rent abated per the
   instrument's terms — automatically, as written, and the record shows it" / where
   none: "never tested, and the term that would have protected you sat in the
   instrument the whole time."]**
4. **Every term was readable before you signed** — and the completed deal matches the
   read: maintenance, insurance, title timing, all as documented.
5. **Title via the separate promise, now executed:** the transfer paperwork per your
   state's cleared form is **[enclosed/at the counter]** — free and clear.
6. **The exit-by-default, honored:** **[incubator drivers: "the arrangement that
   bridged you was chartered to END — today its sunset fires on schedule, your own
   authority activates through the same visible gates as your onboarding, and the
   release is the default doing its job, not a favor being granted. A bridge with an
   end, as promised — this is the end."]**

**What changes now:** you're an owner-operator — your class in the co-op shifts with
it, tenders route to YOUR authority, and the envelope you sign next is a carrier's.
The stake you accrued as a driver continues; nothing resets.

**What doesn't change:** the network is still voluntary, load by load, forever — you
own the truck; nobody owns your yes. The relay legs are there when they serve you and
absent from your obligations when they don't.

**What didn't go right along the way, from us first:**

- **[The honest list across the whole term — the payment-processing hiccup in month
  [n], the maintenance dispute and how the instrument's terms resolved it, the
  abatement event's friction. A multi-year deal accumulates friction; name all of
  it. If genuinely none: the humility, term edition.]**

**No ask.** Not even the obvious one — if a second truck ever makes sense, that
conversation starts with you, on the same terms discipline as the first, and this
letter isn't its opening. Today closes cleanly or it doesn't close.

**[Founding-era drivers / the personal line, where true: "You signed this when it was
a design document and a stranger's promise that it wasn't a fleece. The record above
is what your skepticism built — every driver who signs after you signs something
proven."]**

With respect — and congratulations, owner,

**[CO-OP GOVERNANCE / DEAL ADMINISTRATOR — names]**
**[contact]**

## Usage notes (internal — do not send)

- **The two-column check is the letter's gate**: payment record vs origination
  schedule, verified byte-for-byte before drafting. ANY drift is not a miss to
  footnote — it's a scandal to resolve before this letter exists.
- **The six contrasts audited line by line** against the record; each is stated as
  fact only where the record proves it. An untested protection (item 3's clean
  case) is reported as untested — never as vindicated.
- **Title mechanics per counsel's state-specific cleared form** — the waʿd execution
  is legal paperwork; this letter accompanies it, never substitutes for it.
- **The incubator sunset (item 6)** must fire by DEFAULT in the record — a sunset
  that needed a meeting to happen is a miss even if it happened; the exit-by-default
  was the promise (04 §6's whole point).
- **Class transition mechanics** handled on the log (membership records; the
  driver-class account continues per 1B — accounts per (member, entity), his
  history intact).
- **The funders' closure** (the deal's capital + declared ratio) is their own
  accounting per the instrument's terms — noted to governance, never mixed into
  the driver's letter.
- **NO second-truck language** — the no-re-pitch rule at its most tempting moment.
  The letter's last section says why in the driver's own register.
- **The precedent is the program's**: first completed graduation reported to
  governance as aggregate fact — and to the ruling organ if any instrument condition
  carried a review_date. Every future graduate's trust compounds on this letter
  being exactly true.
