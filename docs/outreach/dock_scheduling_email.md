# Dock-manager scheduling email — template (the window-listing walkthrough)

*Companion to `dock_manager_onepager.md`; books the twenty minutes it asked for.
Persona edges: her day runs in appointment windows — **schedule into her slow one, and
say so**; and she's the champion, not the signer — this email books HER time and
leaves the listing paperwork for whoever holds the facility's pen, through her if she
chooses. Boundary to keep crisp: her war stories inform the work informally; nothing
goes on any log unless she formally consents (the ceremony exists — use it or don't
collect). Zero numbers.*

---

**Subject:** Twenty minutes at your docks — I'll take your slowest window
*(alt: "[CARRIER first name]'s introduction — the walkthrough, on your schedule")*

Hi **[first name]**,

Following up on **[the channel — "[CARRIER]'s introduction" / "our conversation at
the dock"]**. I'd like the twenty minutes we talked about, and since your day runs in
windows, I'll fit whichever one is quietest — you name it, I'm there, and when the
window closes I'm gone.

What happens in those twenty minutes, exactly:

- **The walk**: doors, hours, scheduling quirks, constraints — facility facts, not a
  grading visit. I write down what makes your building run; nothing about it becomes
  a score.
- **Your war stories, if you'll tell them**: the no-shows, the 9-a.m.-arrival
  detention fights, where paperwork sticks. They shape what gets built — informally,
  over coffee, nothing recorded anywhere unless you ever choose to make it formal
  (there's a proper consent process for that, and it's yours to invoke or ignore).
- **Two things on my laptop, five minutes each**: a detention line item carrying its
  signed evidence — so you see what "attestation or it doesn't invoice" actually looks
  like from the receiving side — and the measurement view, showing exactly what a
  facility sees of its own numbers. You'd see what we see; that's the deal, and you
  should watch it before believing it.

What it commits you or the facility to: **nothing.** Window listing is a separate,
later conversation that needs whoever signs for the building — I'd want your read on
who that is and whether it's worth their time, and I'll take "not yet" or "not ever"
without a second ask.

## Usage notes (internal — do not send)

- **Fit her windows, literally.** Arriving during her crunch is the persona-specific
  way to fail this email. "When the window closes I'm gone" is a promise — keep it to
  the minute.
- **Champion, not signer:** do not bring listing paperwork to this meeting. The
  signer conversation happens later, routed through her judgment ("your read on who
  that is") — pushing paper at the wrong desk burns the champion.
- **The war-story boundary:** informal stories inform design informally. If any of it
  should become formal findings (post-harness, this is rare), the FULL consent
  ceremony applies — her key, her classes, her call. Never a shortcut, never notes
  that pretend to be casual but get typed up later.
- **The two demos rehearsed** (the same ten-minute bar as the shipper's verify demo):
  a real evidence-bound line item, and the telemetry basis view. If member freight
  already touches her facility, be ready for "am I being measured right now?" — the
  honest answer is yes-where-freight-flows, floored, and here's your basis, which is
  exactly what the one-pager disclosed.
- **Facility facts captured** (doors, hours, constraints) are commons-class — factual,
  neutral, and shown to her if she asks what was written.
- **Decline handling:** one thank-you; the facility stays a well-treated counterparty
  regardless (her docks serve member freight either way — the relationship is the
  asset, the listing was only ever optional).
