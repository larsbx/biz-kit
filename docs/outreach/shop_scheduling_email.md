# Shop-owner scheduling email — template (the referral-flow walkthrough)

*Companion to `shop_onepager.md`; books the twenty minutes it asked for. Persona
courtesy, built in: his revenue is the bay — **the visit must never cost him a job**,
and the email says so. His capability list is HIS declaration (graded later by signed
work, never by the visitor's opinion) — go to listen, not to upsell. Same boundaries
as the dock email: informal stories inform informally; the consent ceremony or nothing
goes on a log. Zero numbers, no volume promises.*

---

**Subject:** Twenty minutes at your shop — and if a job lands, I'll wait
*(alt: "[CARRIER first name] says you're who he calls — the visit we mentioned")*

Hi **[first name]**,

**[CARRIER first name]** gave me your name the way that matters — as the shop he
calls when it's real. Following up on that: I'd like twenty minutes at your place,
scheduled around your bays, not mine. If work rolls in while I'm there, take it —
I'll wait in the lot or we rebook, no explanation needed.

What the twenty minutes is:

- **You tell me what your bays do best.** That list — your capabilities, in your
  words — is what the network would route work against. Nobody grades it going in;
  it earns its reputation from finished jobs, signed at the time, not from stars or
  my impressions.
- **How work finds you today, and the jobs you turn away that you shouldn't have
  to.** The feast-or-famine mechanics, from your side of the counter — that's design
  input for how routing should work, told over coffee, written down nowhere unless
  you ever choose the formal route (there's a proper process; it's yours to ignore).
- **Five minutes on what membership does and doesn't do**, mostly doesn't: it never
  buys your labor, never touches your prices, never asks for exclusivity. Dues buy
  access and the member rate — never a cut of your work. **[Post-flow: I can show
  you the member demand near you, live, on the laptop.] [Pre-flow: demand near you
  isn't routing yet — your answers are part of building it right, and nothing is
  promised until it's real.]**

What it commits you to: **nothing.** A capability listing afterward is optional and
free; delisting is one message; and whether members keep calling you directly the way
[CARRIER] already does is — as ever — entirely between you and them.

Name a slow morning, or here are three of mine:

- **[option 1 — day, time]**
- **[option 2]**
- **[option 3]**

Thanks, **[first name]** —

**[FOUNDER — name]**
**[phone]**

## Usage notes (internal — do not send)

- **Bays first, literally**: if a customer shows, you wait or leave — the visit that
  cost him a brake job is the visit that killed the relationship. Say it in the
  email, keep it in the lot.
- **Capabilities are his declarations.** Don't suggest what his shop "should" list or
  upsell services — the network grades by signed work after the fact; your job today
  is transcription, not consulting.
- **No volume promises** — if asked "how much work would I see," the answer is the
  mechanism (capability + track record + member demand in your area), never a number.
  Same discipline as every page.
- **The parts/spread demo** (group purchasing with the audit visible) only if that
  program is live — otherwise it's a designed-and-dormant honesty line, not a demo.
- **The story/consent boundary**: identical to the dock email — casual stays casual;
  the ceremony exists for anything formal; no notes that launder coffee talk into
  findings.
- **Membership vs listing are separate conversations** — listing routes work;
  membership adds the vote, the rate, and the buying program. Don't bundle them;
  bundling reads as a catch.
- **Decline handling**: one thank-you, park it. A shop that says no loses nothing it
  had — members keep using whoever they trust (their edges, their calls), and the
  door stays open without a sequence behind it.
