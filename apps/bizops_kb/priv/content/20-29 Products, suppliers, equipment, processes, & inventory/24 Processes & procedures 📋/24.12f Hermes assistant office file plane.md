# Hermes assistant office file plane

How a client company's files move between office devices and our cloud edge,
per department. Part of [[24.12 Hermes assistant on-site deployment]].

## Decision

**Every department gets four Syncthing folders; who holds each one is fixed by
role.** The configuration for every device, office and cloud alike, is generated
from one per-company topology in `native-deployment-control-plane`
(`ops/tenant-edge/tenants.nix`), so the two sides cannot disagree. Nobody edits
Syncthing settings by hand on a device; changes go through the support contact.

| Folder | Anchors | Department desktops | Department laptops | Cloud edge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Company-wide (e.g. `kb`, `icm`) | send + receive | all desktops: send + receive | all laptops: receive | receive, if published as a site |
| `<dept>-open`, `<dept>-internal` | send + receive | send + receive | receive | receive, if published as a site |
| `<dept>-restricted` | send + receive, inside the encrypted vault | — | — | never |
| `<dept>-intake` | librarian anchor only | — | — | send + receive |

- **Anchors** hold every department, the vault included; one of them is the
  **librarian**.
- **Laptops** only receive: they leave the office, so they never push changes.
  Laptop users add documents through upload links or Buzz.
- **Restricted** folders live inside each anchor's encrypted vault and travel only
  between anchors. They never reach a non-anchor desktop or laptop, or the cloud.
- **Intake** is how documents sent through Buzz or Hermes reach the office: Hermes
  drops them in the edge's outbox, they sync to the librarian anchor, the librarian
  files each into the right department tier and removes it from intake.
- **The edge** receives only folders published as wiki or ICM sites, and never
  sends on them.

## Network rules

- Cloud ↔ office connections use the company's own tailnet. Office devices
  may also discover peers and sync directly on the office LAN.
- Global discovery, relays, NAT traversal, crash and usage reporting, and
  auto-upgrade are off on every device.
- The Syncthing web GUI listens only on the device itself, and is off on the edge.

**Removing a machine from Tailscale alone does not revoke Syncthing access.**
Office peers can still accept its device ID over the LAN. The cutoff is removal
of that ID from every remaining peer, with active connections closed, as below.

## Enrolling and removing devices

A device's Syncthing ID exists only once Syncthing has run on it, so enrollment
is a round trip:

1. Install Syncthing and Tailscale on the device (the Windows installer does
   both); its tailnet hostname is its device name.
2. Send its tailnet hostname, Syncthing device ID, machine role (`anchor`,
   `desktop`, or `laptop`), and department(s) to the support contact. For an
   anchor, also state whether it is the librarian; its replicas cover every
   department regardless of the employee's own department(s).
3. The support contact adds it to the topology, regenerates, and returns new
   office `config.xml` files: for this device **and every office device it
   shares a folder with**. The support contact also deploys any affected
   cloud-edge config.
4. The internal admin installs each returned office config with Syncthing
   stopped, keeping the device's `cert.pem` and `key.pem`.
   Windows: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Syncthing`.
5. The admin restarts the updated office devices and checks their folders and
   modes against `matrix.md` using the role-specific handoff checks below.
   The support contact restarts and verifies the updated cloud edge and
   confirms its expected shares before enrollment is complete.

**Remove a device: Syncthing cutoff before tailnet removal**

Arrange the regenerated configs with the support contact before the planned
cutoff. For a lost device, begin the cutoff immediately.

1. Identify all departing Syncthing IDs and every remaining device configured
   to trust them using the roster, matrix, and installed device lists, including
   anchors, desktops, laptops, and the cloud edge. Ask the support contact to
   confirm the affected-device list; do not wait for new configs to begin the
   cutoff in step 2.
2. The admin stops Syncthing on every affected remaining office peer; the
   support contact stops it on the affected cloud edge and confirms that
   active connections have closed. Keep each affected peer stopped, with
   automatic restart disabled, until its new config is installed. The admin
   isolates an offline or unreachable office peer from both LAN and tailnet
   before it can reconnect with its old config: block its switch/Wi-Fi access
   and revoke its tailnet access until it can be updated. The support contact
   handles any required edge isolation. Being offline alone is not isolation;
   otherwise the cutoff is incomplete.
3. The support contact removes the departing IDs from the topology and returns
   regenerated office configs. The admin installs them while Syncthing is
   stopped, preserving each retained office device's own keys. The support
   contact updates the edge config and generated deployment source, so a
   restart cannot restore the old ID.
4. The admin restarts only updated office peers; the support contact restarts
   only the updated edge. Each verifies the departed IDs are absent from the
   devices and folder-sharing lists they manage, with no active connections.
   Support sends the edge verification or isolation evidence to the admin.
   Record each peer as updated and checked, or isolated pending update. If
   configs are delayed, keep affected peers stopped and isolated and proceed
   to step 5 only after confirming every stale peer's isolation, including
   support's edge confirmation. Complete steps 3–4 before releasing that
   isolation or resuming sync.
5. Once every affected peer is updated and checked or isolated, remove the
   departing machines from Tailscale. With a test device, confirm sync fails
   over both the office LAN and tailnet; for a lost device, record the peer-side
   checks and isolation evidence instead. Log the cutoff time and verifier.

Revocation stops further sync; it does not erase files already on the departing
device. Follow [[24.12a Hermes assistant admin runbook]] for account removal,
upload-link revocation, and key rotation.

A department or machine-role change also goes through the support contact.
Withdraw the old folder shares on all affected peers before enabling the new
ones; do not treat tailnet tags alone as the folder-permission change.

## Checks before handoff

- [ ] The folder × device matrix (`matrix.md` for this company) reviewed with the owner
- [ ] Each non-anchor desktop or laptop has the company-wide folders plus only its assigned departments' open/internal folders; desktops send + receive, laptops receive only
- [ ] Each anchor, including an employee-used anchor, holds every department's open/internal/restricted folders and the company-wide folders; restricted folders stay inside its vault
- [ ] Department intake folders are shared only between the librarian anchor and the edge
- [ ] Restricted folders present only on anchors, inside the mounted vault
- [ ] A test document sent through Buzz reached the librarian's intake and was filed
- [ ] Admin demonstrated a device revocation; peer-side cutoff and failed LAN/tailnet sync recorded before handoff
