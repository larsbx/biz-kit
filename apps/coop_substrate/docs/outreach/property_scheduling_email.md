# Property-owner scheduling email — template (the land co-op reading session)

*Companion to `property_owner_onepager.md` and gated identically — **plus more**: under
a securities exemption, even sending this email is a regulated act. Counsel's
distribution rules govern WHO may receive it, HOW, and what RECORD of delivery is
kept; the bracket below is for language counsel inserts, not for you to fill. Three
hygiene rules unique to this email: **no signing at the first meeting, by design** ·
**no urgency language, ever** (founding windows exist elsewhere; shares never get
scarcity framing) · **no numbers, no returns talk, in writing or otherwise.** When in
doubt about any sentence, the sentence waits for counsel.*

**Subject:** The reading session we discussed — at the yard, if you like
*(alt: "The land co-op documents — a slow read, no decisions")*

Dear **[first name]**,

Following our conversation **[context — at the chapter meeting / through your
membership]**, I'd like to schedule the session I promised: a slow read of the land
co-op's documents, with the asset in view if you want it — **we can meet at the
[parcel/yard] itself**, so the thing the paper describes is the thing you're standing
on. Ground you can walk beats paper you have to decode; that's rather the point of
the whole structure.

What the session is, plainly:

1. **The documents, read together, slowly**: the redemption gates (declared before
   anyone buys), the appraisal protocol (independent, recorded, with the
   second-appraiser path), the concentration ceiling, and **the risk paragraph —
   which we read in full, because it's true**: value can fall, redemptions can queue,
   income isn't guaranteed, there is no market to sell into.
2. **The membership conversation** — membership precedes any share in everything this
   co-op does, and it's a decision on its own merits.
3. **Your hardest questions**, especially about the appraisal basis. Written answers
   exist; where one doesn't, "that isn't decided yet" is the answer you'll get, in
   those words.

And one rule I want you to know going in: **nothing can be signed at this meeting.**
That's not caution about you — it's policy for everyone. You take every document home,
you sit with them as long as you like, and any next step happens later, through the
formal process, at your initiation. If anyone ever rushes you toward shares in
anything — here or elsewhere — that urgency is itself the information.

## Usage notes (internal — do not send)

- **Distribution is the regulated act**: send only to recipients counsel's exemption
  rules permit, by the means counsel specifies, and **keep the delivery record
  counsel requires** (offeree logs are commonly mandatory). One recipient per email;
  no forwarding request; if he forwards it himself, note it for counsel.
- **The counsel block is load-bearing** — this template is incomplete by construction
  until counsel resolves it. An email sent with the bracket still in it is a
  compliance incident, not a typo.
- **No-decision-first-meeting is policy, stated in the email** — securities hygiene
  and trust hygiene are the same hygiene here. Bring documents to leave behind,
  never documents to sign.
- **No urgency, no scarcity, no "others are interested"** — ever, in any channel.
  The founding-window language used elsewhere in this kit must never appear near
  shares.
- **Questions answered in writing where possible** — written answers are consistent
  answers, and consistency is both the exemption's friend and the member's.
- **The venue offer** (meet at the parcel) is genuine but optional — his choice; some
  prefer the kitchen table, and the documents read the same at both.
- **If he asks about returns or income numbers**: the utilization-dependent,
  not-guaranteed answer from the one-pager, verbatim — and note that the question
  was asked, for counsel's file.
- **Decline handling**: permanently gracious, zero follow-up sequence, and his
  membership (if any) is entirely unaffected — shares were always optional on top of
  it, never the price of belonging.
