# Corridor go-live checklist — the first relay leg

*The activation checklist's ground-level companion: that document opens the locks;
this one runs the first leg — one named driver, one real load, one bed — as the
ceremony it is. Because the first relay leg is the driver theorem's first data
point: after tonight, "home daily" is either a count that starts 1-for-1 or a
promise that broke on contact, and every driver this corridor ever recruits will
be told which. The Face checklist's altitude rule applies verbatim: **one leg,
done exactly right, beats a launch week** — and one absolute travels with it from
the driver documents: **never contact him while he's rolling.***

---

## 0 — Preconditions

- [ ] `corridor_activation_checklist.md` CLOSED: every lock open, the
      `corridor/<A>-<B>/active` constant on the log — read it back, don't
      remember it
- [ ] In particular (the two that hurt if assumed): the relay-leg liability
      terms attached to THIS leg's yard boundaries, and `daily_range` declared —
      the assignment below is unevaluatable without it
- [ ] The rehearsal drop-and-hook from activation §3 verified on the log — the
      custody muscle memory exists before real freight tests it

## 1 — The load (real, not staged)

- [ ] The first leg carries whatever freight is actually moving — **no showcase
      load arranged to make the numbers pretty**; the corridor's first data
      point is only worth having if it's real (the full-stream rule applied to
      night one)
- [ ] The load's tender and routing per the ordinary rails: signed, in-envelope,
      evidence-bound — nothing bespoke for the occasion
- [ ] Both yards' staging confirmed operable TODAY (the fail-closed predicate,
      fresh — not the activation-day reading)

## 2 — The driver (his yes, mechanically his)

- [ ] His own yes to this leg, per the constitution: voluntary, this leg,
      no duty to the next one — said in words at assignment, not just true in
      the type system
- [ ] His envelope covers the leg; the assignment gate evaluated in the open:
      **leg length ≤ daily_range, checked by the system that cannot assign
      otherwise** — show him the check passing; it's HIS theorem tonight
- [ ] HOS feasibility per the accept check, from the declared data source —
      never from optimism
- [ ] The geometry sheet in his hand (activation §5's page, now personal): his
      leg's length, the declared range, where the handoff yard sits, who takes
      the trailer from there
- [ ] The contact rule set BEFORE he rolls: who he calls parked, and the
      standing absolute — **nobody calls him rolling, tonight of all nights**

## 3 — The leg (every link, first time)

- [ ] Origin hook: dual-signed, condition-attested — the rehearsed pattern, now
      with freight in it
- [ ] The drive: NOTHING — no check-calls (the node is deleted, not suspended
      for launch nerves), no tracking theater; the evidence model holds at the
      moment it's most tempting to hover
- [ ] Far-yard drop: dual-signed, condition-attested; if this relay hands to a
      second driver tonight, HIS leg runs this same checklist's §2 — the second
      driver is not a footnote to the first
- [ ] Dwell at both yards: logged by the events, watched same-day (the fold,
      not a stopwatch)

## 4 — The bed (the theorem's first data point)

- [ ] He sleeps at home tonight — and the record can show it: leg events +
      home-terminal arrival; **the bed count starts 1-for-1 or the miss is
      named tonight**, with its cause, in the corridor block (a leg that fit
      on paper and still cost the night is OUR variance to explain, the trial
      letter's contract already in force)
- [ ] Tomorrow, parked, at his pace: the waiting question asked — how the yards
      treated him, how the handoff felt, what he was told while he waited.
      Network conduct is ours to fix; his employment stays behind its wall,
      tonight and always

## 5 — Same-day after

- [ ] Chains verified (`Log.verify_chains` → :ok); cross-chapter: mirrored
      attestations reference each other's hashes, both logs checked
- [ ] The weekly status corridor block opens with night one: legs 1, beds ____,
      exceptions ____ — the count that every future driver letter will quote
      begins honestly
- [ ] Misses-first, leg-one edition, written tonight: the hook that ran late,
      the yard queue, the assignment friction — the driver's own answers lead
      the list when he gives them tomorrow
- [ ] Escalations to the R-queue, assigned not admired; the driver trial
      letter's clock starts (it owns the reckoning after the first WEEKS — this
      checklist owns only night one)
- [ ] The detention-category evidence starts accruing: staged, coverage-cleared
      endpoints removing live-load detention is now a claim with a data point —
      exceptions as logged events, never silently

## 6 — What does NOT happen tonight (refusals, verified)

- [ ] No call to the cab — if something goes wrong mid-leg, it goes wrong until
      he parks; the plan for that was made before he rolled
- [ ] No cherry-picked announcement: whatever is said about leg one resolves to
      the folds, bed count included, miss included if there is one
- [ ] No second-leg pressure on the driver's yes: "the relay earns your
      seat-time or it doesn't deserve it" is policy, not ad copy — his next
      leg is his next choice
- [ ] No employment questions in the debrief — the wall, on night one, sets
      the precedent for every debrief after
