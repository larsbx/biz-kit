# Yard-owner scheduling email — template (the staging walkthrough)

*Companion to `yard_owner_onepager.md`; schedules the twenty-minute lot walk it asked
for. The walkthrough's double life, by design: it captures the facility facts (gates,
surface, lighting, hours, constraints), and **if a member attends — bring one, or be
one — the same visit is the security attestation** that loaded staging will later
require. One visit, both jobs, and the owner watches both happen. Zero numbers, and
one discipline unique to this persona: NEVER suggest a per-drop rate — the terms are
theirs to declare, full stop.*

---

Hi **[first name]**,

Following up on **[where — "our talk at the property" / "[CARRIER]'s introduction"]**:

Here's exactly what happens during it, so nothing's a surprise:

- **We walk it together** — gates, surface, lighting, fence line, where trailers could
  sit without touching your other arrangements. I'm noting facts, not grading you:
  hours that work, constraints that matter, what's off-limits.
- **[If member attending:] [MEMBER first name], a co-op member carrier, comes along —**
  because before any *loaded* trailer could ever sit on your ground, the system
  requires a member to have walked the lot and attested the fences and lighting.
  Doing it now means your listing starts with that box already checked, and you hear
  exactly what was attested, standing right there.
- **You tell me your terms.** Per-drop rate, hours, any conditions — yours to name,
  in writing, revisable. I won't be suggesting numbers; that's not humility, it's the

unlisting is one message; nothing stages until the insurance check passes in software
and staging demand actually reaches your area — and per our own rules, **we can't sign
a lease until member demand for your specific lot is already pledged**, so if a
signature ever lands on your table, the trucks behind it are real.

## Usage notes (internal — do not send)

- **Bring the attesting member or be one** — a walkthrough without the attestation
  wastes the owner's second twenty minutes later. The attestation is the member's
  honest observation, signed; never coach it (a false attestation is a censure event
  against the attester, and it should be).
- **The security-bits walk:** fences, lighting, surface, gate control, sight lines —
  captured as facility facts (commons class). Photograph NOTHING without explicit
  permission; it's their property and the relationship is the asset.
- **Never anchor a rate.** If they ask "what do lots usually get," the honest answer
  is that terms are owner-declared across the network and you won't be the one who
  anchored theirs. If they name a number, note it as THEIR opening declaration —
  yours to accept into a listing, never to negotiate per-truck later (per-call
  haggling is structurally dead here; that protects them too).
- **No activation dates.** "When demand pledges and coverage binds" is the complete
  answer — the pre/post-corridor bracket from the one-pager governs tone.
- **Existing tenants:** ask, respect, and note constraints — their month-to-month
  arrangements are theirs; the listing coexists or it doesn't list.
- **Decline handling:** one thank-you, park it; a lot that says no stays a good
  neighbor and never becomes data (prospect records only under the tracker's private
  discipline; nothing on any log without a listing).
