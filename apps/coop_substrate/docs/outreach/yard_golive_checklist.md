# Yard go-live checklist — the first staged trailer

*Third of the first-night ceremonies (the Face's load, the corridor's leg, now the
yard's trailer). The activation checklist opened the yard's locks; this one runs
the first real drop on her asphalt — and its stakes are the yard pitch itself:
the condition report was the one-pager's whole answer to "what happens when
somebody dings a trailer on my lot," so **the first drop's paperwork is the
product**. Two absolutes, held hardest under schedule pressure: **no loaded
trailer stages on a stale security attestation** (Rosa's gate has no
just-this-once), and **not one word about shares while standing on her ground**
(the anti-bait restraint, on-site edition). One leg-one rule inherited: real
freight, no showcase.*

---

## 0 — Preconditions

- [ ] The listing live on the log: her declared terms — per-drop card, gate
      hours, contact rules — as SHE stated them (her words, not our summary);
      read them back before scheduling anything
- [ ] `operable(yard)` true TODAY — the fail-closed predicate, fresh within τ,
      not the activation-day reading
- [ ] **Loaded freight ⇒ G2 fresh**: the member-walked security attestation
      (fences, lighting) within its freshness window — if it staled between
      activation and tonight, the walk happens again BEFORE the drop, whatever
      that does to the schedule
- [ ] Liability terms for staged trailers attached and insurer disclosure done
      (activation §4's discipline, verified for THIS yard) — a trailer parked
      on unassigned liability is the lawsuit-with-yards line, singular
- [ ] The custody rehearsal from activation verified on the log — tonight is
      the pattern's first real test, not its first run

## 1 — The trailer (real, and inside her rules)

- [ ] Real freight on its real schedule — no showcase drop staged to
      christen the lot
- [ ] The drop scheduled INSIDE her declared gate hours — the first drop
      arriving at 2 a.m. against a posted 22:00 close would spend the
      relationship's whole trust budget on night one; if freight timing and
      her hours don't fit, the drop waits or the conversation happens first,
      at her initiative to flex, never ours to presume
- [ ] Who drops and who hooks: named drivers, both briefed on her lot's rules
      (speed, parking lanes, where not to idle) — her ground, her manners,
      their conduct OUR accountability

## 2 — The drop (the paperwork is the product)

- [ ] At the gate: the dual-signed condition report, at the moment, both
      signatures — photos per the pattern; the report references the trailer,
      the timestamp, the lot
- [ ] **Her copy delivered on the spot** — the pitch was that the report
      protects HER; a report she has to ask for protects the network first
      and her second, which is the wrong order
- [ ] The chain verified same-day (`Log.verify_chains` → :ok) — the drop event
      where it belongs, signed by whom it claims

## 3 — The dwell (the quiet part, done honestly)

- [ ] The trailer sits per the terms — dwell tracked by the fold, not by
      calling her to ask; **her lot is not surveilled**: what the record holds
      is drop, hook, and condition, nothing about her gate traffic, her other
      customers, her cameras
- [ ] Any mid-dwell event (a move on the lot, an incident) gets the same
      pattern: attested, dual-signed where two parties touch, her copy on the
      spot
- [ ] The hook out: dual-signed condition report again, closing the loop —
      the pair of reports is now the dispute protection the one-pager
      promised, existing for real

## 4 — The payment (the number that sets the reputation)

- [ ] Per-drop payment at HER card — the declared rate, no launch-week
      renegotiation in either direction
- [ ] **Paid on time, per the terms, externally, attested on the rail** — the
      first payment's punctuality is the fact she'll repeat at the county
      line; a slipping date reaches her BEFORE she notices it (the standing
      rule, live from payment one)

## 5 — Same-day after

- [ ] Her debrief, at her pace: gate hours honored? Driver conduct? Anything
      about the drop that didn't match the walkthrough? Network conduct is
      ours to fix — and her answers lead the misses list
- [ ] Misses-first, drop-one edition, written tonight: the arrival that ran
      late against her window, the report that took two tries, the driver who
      idled where he shouldn't
- [ ] The weekly status yard block opens: drops 1, hooks ____, condition
      exceptions ____, payment on-time 1/1 — the counts her trial letter will
      quote begin honestly
- [ ] The yard trial letter's clock starts (`yard_trial_followup.md` owns the
      weeks; tonight owns tonight)
- [ ] Escalations to the R-queue, assigned not admired

## 6 — What does NOT happen tonight (refusals, verified)

- [ ] **No loaded trailer on a stale G2** — schedule pressure is exactly when
      Rosa's gate earns its name; the freight waits or the walk happens
- [ ] **No shares talk on her asphalt** — not a word, not a "by the way,"
      tonight or any site visit; the reading-session process exists, at her
      initiation, elsewhere (the disclosed-once logic holds hardest standing
      on the thing she might one day contribute)
- [ ] No gate-hours exception requested "just tonight" — her windows were the
      deal; night one is when the deal proves it meant itself
- [ ] No minimums implied in either direction: this drop promises no next
      drop, and asks no promise back — the no-commitments symmetry restated
      in words if the moment invites it
- [ ] No announcement beyond the folds: one trailer staged, both reports
      signed, one payment on time — counts, not adjectives
