# Chapter go-live day checklist — the mirrored handoff evening, orchestrated

*The bilateral sibling of `golive_day_checklist.md`: that sheet conducts one
chapter's checklists on one evening; this one conducts an evening SPLIT ACROSS A
SOVEREIGNTY BOUNDARY — two coordinators, two logs, two sheet-sets, and sync
points where neither side may see the other's internals. The coordination
currency is therefore fixed up front: **each side gives the other its YES, its
HASHES, and its COUNTS — never its checklists, its members' names beyond the
fence pair, or its internal state.** This sheet adds no items to any checklist
and waives none; `chapter_golive_checklist.md` owns the handoff itself, and
each chapter's other sheets (yard, driver, corridor) own their own boxes.
Checklist wins, both sides of the border.*

---

## 0 — The week before (jointly agreed, separately executed)

- [ ] Each chapter names ITS coordinator — one per side, each conducting only
      their own chapter's sheets; neither directs, checks, or reads the
      other's
- [ ] The evening's sheet-set agreed per side: `chapter_golive_checklist.md`
      both sides, plus whichever apply locally (the boundary yard's sheet on
      the listing side; a driver sheet where a first solo coincides; the
      corridor sheet if this is also leg one)
- [ ] **The bilateral rehearsal done** (the chapter checklist's §0 item) with
      BOTH coordinators present at the dry run — the ordering, the hash
      channel, and the "green" exchange below all rehearsed as one motion
- [ ] The evening's channel agreed: how the two coordinators exchange the
      three currencies (yes/no · hashes · counts) and NOTHING else — and the
      standing rule stated at agreement time: a question about the other
      side's internals doesn't get asked, so it never has to get refused
- [ ] The failure-branch scripts (§4 below) read aloud together once — the
      only part of each other's evening either side should know by heart

## 1 — Sync point one: the two greens

- [ ] Each side runs its own §0s ON THE DAY (freshness rules apply per sheet)
- [ ] Each coordinator sends the other ONE WORD when their side is fully
      green — **the yes carries no attachments**: not which boxes, not what
      almost failed, not who fixed what; "we are green" is a sovereign act of
      responsibility, and taking it at face value is the treaty working
- [ ] Both greens in hand → the handoff window opens; either green missing at
      the agreed time → branch A, no negotiation

## 2 — Sync point two: the fence (the chapter checklist owns this — the
orchestration just keeps time)

- [ ] The handoff and mirror per `chapter_golive_checklist.md` §§1–2: the
      releasing side's append, the hash across the fence, the receiving
      side's mirror — the ONE moment the two evenings touch, and it touches
      through a hash
- [ ] Each coordinator tracks only their own side's clock on the mirror-close
      target; if the mirror is slow, each side works its own half — nobody
      troubleshoots the other's log, even by suggestion

## 3 — Sync point three: the same-day cluster (parallel, then one exchange)

- [ ] Each side verifies its own half (chains, the resolving cross-reference)
      per the chapter checklist §3
- [ ] **Checkpoints emitted both ends and EXCHANGED** — the evening's second
      and last treaty touch: two sealed heads crossing the border, the
      mutual-witness clause getting its mid-cycle beat
- [ ] Each side's counts confirmed to the other: handoff 1, mirror closed 1/1,
      disputes 0 — counts as the third currency, closing the evening's
      ledger between the governances

## 4 — Failure branches (bilateral editions — scripted because a border
makes improvisation worse)

- **A. One side isn't green:** the evening postpones. The not-green side says
  "not tonight," and owes NOTHING further — no reason, no timeline, no
  apology; the other side asks nothing (the zero-probing rule at chapter
  scale). Both sides' freight plans unwind per their own operations. "A gate
  held in one of two sovereign chapters" is the founding story, and it's a
  good one.
- **B. The physical handoff completes but the mirror won't close** (an append
  fails, a channel drops): the PAPER is the record — both signatures, both
  copies, the one-pattern's whole point; the mirror closes when it closes,
  with honest timestamps, and the delay leads that side's misses. Neither
  side backfills, antedates, or "helps" the other's append.
- **C. The condition facts disagree at the fence** (the reports don't match
  before anyone signs): nothing signs until they do — the dispute machinery's
  first test happens BEFORE the mirror, at the fence, between the two members,
  the way every future dispute should: by looking at the trailer together.
- **D. Any branch fires:** no handoff two until BOTH sides' misses lists
  exist and each has fixed its own half — the no-velocity rule, requiring two
  sovereign yeses to resume, same as it took two to start.

## 5 — The debrief split (the evening's last discipline)

- [ ] Each chapter debriefs its own member, writes its own misses list, files
      its own governance aggregate — per the chapter checklist §4, untouched
- [ ] The coordinators' own debrief is also SPLIT: each writes what the
      orchestration got wrong on their side; the two exchange aggregates only
      (counts and categories), and only if both choose
- [ ] The 09 §1 data point filed from each side independently — two
      perspectives on the same friction is exactly what the joint agenda
      wants; merging them beforehand would launder the disagreement out
- [ ] Both follow-up cadences start on their own logs; the next checkpoint
      exchange lands on the agreed cadence, back to ordinary

## 6 — Orchestration-level refusals (the union, plus the border's own)

- [ ] Every underlying checklist's refusals verified by its own side's runner
- [ ] **The three-currencies rule held all evening**: yes/no, hashes, counts —
      anything else that crossed the border tonight goes on the misses list
      of whoever sent it
- [ ] No joint war-room, no shared dashboard, no "one screen with both logs" —
      the evening is coordinated, not merged; two rooms, one fence, one hash
- [ ] No cross-border troubleshooting, tonight or in the debrief — advice is
      interference wearing a helpful face
