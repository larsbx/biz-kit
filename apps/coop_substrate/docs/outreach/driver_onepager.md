## The geometry, not the promise

This network runs freight in **relay**: instead of one driver living in the truck from
origin to destination, the load moves yard to yard in **legs sized to a driver's day**.
That's not a perk bolted on — it's the operating design, because a network drivers
prefer wins the drivers, and freight follows capacity. The rule is written into the
system: *a leg only assigns if it fits the daily range* — and if a leg fits the day,
you sleep at home.

**The honest limit, upfront:** which home-daily legs exist depends on which corridors
are live. **[Pre-corridor: none are yet — this outreach is part of building the first
one, and I'll show you the planned legs for your region and tell you plainly they're
planned, not running.] [Post-corridor: the [corridor] legs run today — I'll show you
the actual leg map for your region, live, not a brochure.]** Where the geometry or the
freight doesn't support home-daily, the system doesn't pretend; it just doesn't
assign you a lie.

## Who you'd actually work for (the part recruiters mumble)

You'd drive for a **member carrier** — normal employment, normal accountability. The
co-op itself **never employs drivers and never dispatches trucks; its constitution
forbids it.** No fleet in disguise, no misclassification games. What the co-op adds
around that normal job: the relay legs, the paperwork done by software instead of your
evenings, and the two things below.

## The stake (and why this isn't a pyramid)

Drivers are a **member class** — one member, one vote, same vote as the owner of twenty
trucks. Your stake accrues from your **participation** — the work you do through the
network — never from recruiting anyone: **nobody here earns a cent for bringing names;
the software can't even record such a payment.** You can watch your own stake on your
own phone, computed from your own record, and nobody else's.

## The path to your own truck (read this one skeptically — we did)

You know the lease-purchase game: miles-based bait, walk-away "options" that eat years
of payments, terms that appear after you've signed. **The graduation instrument here is
structurally the opposite, point by point:**

- **No interest, by construction** — the contract types this system permits cannot
  carry it; that's a property of the instrument, not a marketing line.
- **The buy-down schedule is declared before you sign** — every payment's effect on
  your ownership, in writing, first.
- **The truck is bound to your name** from day one of the deal — there is no pool of
  drivers churning through the same tractor; a pooled unit is literally
  unrepresentable in the system.
- **Until title passes, ownership risk stays on the owner** — if the truck's destroyed,
  the rent stops; that's in the instrument's required terms, not in your prayers.
- **All terms — maintenance, insurance, title timing, early exit, what happens if
  volume drops — live in the instrument you read BEFORE committing.**
- **And the exit-by-default rule:** the arrangement is chartered to *graduate* you to
  your own authority, with a sunset written into it — a bridge with an end, not a
  treadmill.

**Where this stands:** the instrument is designed and written down; **it is offered
nowhere until lawyers clear it for your state — anyone offering you one today isn't
us.** What exists today is the interview below and the path's front end: relay work
for member carriers as corridors go live.

## Getting in (what the law requires, we require — visibly)

Onboarding is one sitting: qualification documents photographed once, parsed in
minutes — **and then the verification the law and safety demand (licensing,
clearinghouse, history) runs before anything activates, no matter how fast the
paperwork went.** You watch each gate clear on your own screen. Nobody rides on
promises here, including us.

## The ask

An hour of your experience first — how you job-search, what you distrust (be brutal;
distrust is design input here), what "home daily" would actually change. Interviews
shape what gets built and come with early-member standing. If the region's legs
interest you after that, we go from there — at the speed of the gates, not the pitch.
