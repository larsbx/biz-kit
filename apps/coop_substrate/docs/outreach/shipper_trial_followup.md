# Shipper trial follow-up — template (after the trial loads)

*Follows `shipper_onepager.md` and the first-lane session; sent after the agreed
handful of trial loads completed. The discipline TRANSFORMS here: for the first time,
real numbers belong in the envelope — because they're **her own receipts**, attached
as independently verifiable exports. What stays forbidden: promises (future rates,
savings projections), aggregate market claims, and volume asks. The deal was
judged-on-receipts; this letter IS the receipts, plus the honest ledger of what went
wrong. **If the trial went badly, use the variant in the notes — never spin.***

---

**Subject:** Your [N] trial loads — the receipts, as promised
*(alt: "Judged on the receipts: here they are")*

Hi **[first name]**,

When we set up the [corridor] lane you agreed to judge us on the receipts. Attached
are all of them:

- **The custody exports for each of your [N] loads** — every signed touch, dock to
  dock where the full line ran through network stages, and marked plainly where a leg
  ran outside them. Your auditors can verify these with no access to our systems;
  the one-page instruction is included, and I'd genuinely rather they check than
  take my word.
- **Quote-to-invoice, load by load** — each invoice against the tender it answered.
  **[Truthfully: "no variances" / "one variance, flagged below."]**
- **Every accessorial line, with its evidence attached** — and I want to point at
  both directions of that, because it's the honest heart of this system:
  **[If applicable: "the detention line your [site] received on load [x] carried its
  signed arrive/depart evidence — the same machinery that protects you from
  unverifiable claims billed you a verifiable one. The record cuts both ways or it's
  worthless, and your site can see its own measurement basis and improve it — that
  symmetry was in the first conversation, and here it is in practice."]**

**What didn't go right, named by us first:**

- **[The honest list: the late leg, the document that took two days, the exception
  and how it surfaced as a logged event rather than a mystery. If truly nothing:
  "genuinely nothing to report — which after [N] loads I'm treating as luck as much
  as design; the next miss will be in the next report."]**

**The ask, if the receipts hold up under your audit:** make the lane routine. Your
credit line already means tenders move without ceremony — "routine" just means you
keep sending them, one load at a time, each its own decision, exactly as now. No
volume commitment asked, because we don't offer one either — the desk keeps earning
loads or it stops getting them, which is the arrangement we'd want in your chair.
**[Where true: "and [corridor B] goes live next — same receipts, when you're ready."]**

What doesn't change: no rate sheet (lanes quote live, the quote is the claim), your
data stays bilateral, and the exit remains what it always was — stop tendering, owe
nothing, part friends.

Thanks for testing us on your hardest lane, **[first name]** —

## Usage notes (internal — do not send)

- **Prepare and verify the exports YOURSELF first** — run the independent
  verification on every attached export before sending. An export that fails
  verification in their auditor's hands is a catastrophe this checklist line
  prevents for free.
- **The misses list is mandatory and comes from you first** — a shipper who finds a
  miss you didn't name learns the reports are marketing; a shipper who reads your
  miss before finding it learns the reports are true. If a leg ran outside network
  stages, the export already says so — say it in prose too.
- **The both-directions detention paragraph**: only where it happened, stated with
  the symmetry framing, never apologetically — her site got a *verifiable* bill,
  which is the upgrade. Have her site's measurement basis ready if she asks.
- **BAD-TRIAL VARIANT**: if the receipts are ugly — name every failure, state what
  changed structurally (not "we'll try harder" — what gate, what envelope, what
  process changed), and offer exactly one more load or accept the no with grace.
  Never send the good-trial letter with soft edges; the register difference is the
  credibility.
- **No volume asks, mirrored**: "routine" is defined in the letter as one-load-at-a-
  time continuing — never convert the trial's success into a commitment request;
  the asymmetry would betray the I2/I5 posture.
- **Numbers discipline, transformed not dropped**: HER loads' figures may appear
  (they're her bilateral data, in her envelope) — aggregate claims, other shippers'
  anything, and forward projections still never.
- **If a dispute is open**: this letter waits until it's resolved or it names the
  dispute and its evidence status honestly — never send receipts-as-triumph over an
  open argument.
