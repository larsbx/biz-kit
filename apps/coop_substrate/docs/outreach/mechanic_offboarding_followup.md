# Worker-member offboarding follow-up — template (when a worker departs)

*The operational twin of the worker redemption letter (#8, which owns her capital —
two letters, never merged, same as the carrier pair). **Sender: the service co-op's
own governance** — the freight co-op gifted the template; the relationship is this
worker co-op's alone. The trailers-off analog is the truck: **handed back,
condition-attested, before this letter sends.** Bad standing changes warmth, never
rights; the grievance rule applies; and one door is named once, factually, because
it's true: a departing mechanic who hangs her own shingle is a shop, and the network
has a door for shops. Series rules; no counter-offers after the lifecycle event.*

---

**Subject:** Your departure, done properly — the handover, your record, and your phone
*(alt: "Filing your exit the right way: what's returned, what's yours, what was always yours")*

Dear **[first name]**,

With your **[departure/retirement]** recorded, this letter closes the operational
side properly — your capital account has its own letter, on its own schedule; nothing
here is about money.

**The handover, attested:** the truck and tools came back **[date]**, with a signed
condition report at the moment — the same one-pattern this co-op uses for everything
that changes hands, protecting you exactly as it protected the fleet: if any question
about the rig's condition ever surfaces, it's a comparison of documents, not
memories. **[Any noted items, factually.]**

**Your export — your working life here, portable:** attached is your complete
record — every call, every job with its signed completion, your qualification
documents, your patronage history, and the declarations needed to reproduce your own
numbers from scratch. **It verifies offline, forever.** Your years here are yours to
carry, in evidence form, wherever the next wrench turns.

**The paperwork:** your final documents per the chartered separation terms — the
same counsel-reviewed shape you read before you signed, now doing its last job —
**[accompany this letter / arrive by [date]]**. Nothing improvised at the exit that
wasn't improvised at the entrance, which was nothing.

**Your phone, said plainly:** the rate card governed this co-op's dispatch — **it
never governed your phone, and it certainly doesn't now.** Who calls you tomorrow,
and what you charge them, is your business as a private tradesperson; nobody here
tracks it, counts it, or gets a say. The 2 a.m. calls were always answered by you;
they were never ours to reassign.

**One door, named once because it's true:** if the next thing is your own truck and
your own book of calls, **the network has a door for independent shops — and the
track record you built here, signed job by signed job, walks in with you** if you
ever want it to. Stated as a fact about a door; nobody will call you about it.

**What survives:** your standing (you leave in good standing, on the record)
**[bad-standing variant: the standing stated factually; every right in this letter —
handover attestation, export, paperwork, the door — identical]** · the record of
your years, sealed and yours · the way back (rejoining is a fresh membership; your
history recommends you).

**What ends:** your vote, with your working membership — the rule the capital letter
says more about, kept brief here: governance belongs to whoever's on the trucks.

**What didn't go right across your years, from us first:**

- **[The honest aggregate — the dispatch friction, the parts delay that ate her
  Saturday, the card revision that landed badly. If she's departing over a
  grievance: it appears here, in her terms, with our answer — a departure letter
  that omits the departure's reason is about a different member.]**

Thank you for every shoulder you rolled to, **[first name]** — the fleet you
part-owned is better for your years in it, and the part of that which pays out is
already on its way, on schedule, in the other envelope.

With respect,

**[SERVICE CO-OP GOVERNANCE — names/roles]**
**[contact]**

## Usage notes (internal to the SERVICE CO-OP's governance — do not send)

- **The truck back BEFORE the letter** — condition-attested handover, dated; the
  trailers-off rule, worker edition. A goodbye with the co-op's truck still in her
  driveway is a negotiation, not a goodbye.
- **The export self-verified before delivery** — calls, jobs, quals, patronage,
  reproduction declarations; the member-export promise is the worker co-op's too,
  and keeping it sloppily is breaking it.
- **Separation paperwork per the chartered, counsel-reviewed terms (Tier 3.4)** —
  the classification honesty from her first email holds at her last: nothing
  improvised, and the tax shape was defined before anyone signed.
- **Her phone is hers — operationally honored**: no tracking of post-departure
  direct calls from members, no follow-up on them, nothing. Members' edges and her
  business, neither of them ours.
- **The shop-door line appears ONCE** — factual, unfollowed, and only where it
  plausibly applies; it names a genuinely different relationship (worker-member →
  independent shop), which is why it survives the no-winback rule. A second
  mention converts a door into a pitch.
- **Two letters, never merged** — operations here, capital in the redemption
  letter, each on its own schedule.
- **Bad-standing and grievance rules** — identical to the carrier offboarding:
  rights don't depend on the exit's mood; the grievance appears in her terms with
  our answer.
- **No counter-offers after the lifecycle event** — retention conversations
  happened before or not at all.
- **The precedent to governance as aggregate** — first worker departure handled
  per the letter is every remaining worker-member's information; her individual
  record stays hers.
