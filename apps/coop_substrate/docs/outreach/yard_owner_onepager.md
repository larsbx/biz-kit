## What this is

A carrier-owned cooperative is setting up **drop-and-hook staging** along the
[corridor A]↔[corridor B] run: trailers dropped on fenced lots near each end, picked
up when the next leg is ready. We're not brokers, not a 3PL, and not looking to run
your property — we're looking to **pay for idle asphalt, per trailer that touches it**.

## The offer shape

- **Per-drop terms.** You're paid per trailer staged, at a rate schedule declared in
  writing before anything moves — no haggling per truck, no invoices to chase down a
  mystery.
- **No term commitment — in either direction.** You're not signing years; we're not
  promising minimums. Honest both ways: we will never guarantee you throughput
  (our rules forbid shifting that risk), and you never guarantee us capacity.
- **Nothing operational transfers.** Your gate, your rules, your other tenants. The
  co-op's constitution forbids it taking operational control of anyone's property or
  equipment — that's an article, not a courtesy.
- **Here's the part that should interest a careful owner:** by our own rules, **we
  can't sign until member demand for your specific lot is already pledged.** When we
  come to sign, the trucks are real — evidence first, signatures second is how this
  whole outfit runs.

## Your two real worries, answered structurally

**"Who pays when a trailer's damaged on my lot?"** Every drop and every hook is signed
by the parties **with a condition report at that moment**. A damage dispute stops being
a memory contest and becomes a comparison of two adjacent reports — the record exists
before anyone's angry.

**"What about liability with loaded trailers sitting there?"** The software **will not
stage a loaded trailer at your lot until the insurance check passes** — coverage for
that class of facility, verified fresh. That's a hard gate in the code, not a policy
memo. And before any loaded freight ever stages, a member walks your lot and attests
the fences and lighting — your listing earns the grade loaded freight requires, and
you'll know exactly what was attested.

## What we're asking today

Twenty minutes and a walk of the lot: gates, surface, lighting, fence line, what hours
work for you. Then, if it suits you, your lot lists — at *your* declared terms — and it
gets matched when staging demand reaches your area. Listing costs nothing and commits
nothing; unlisting is one message.

- **[Pre-activation]** The corridor isn't live yet — that's exactly why we're talking

  owed until it does, and I'll tell you plainly when that changes.
- **[Post-activation]** Trailers are running the [corridor] today. I can show you the
  live demand for your area on my laptop — the numbers exist and they're not mine to
  print on a flyer.

## What we will never do

No throughput guarantees (both ways — see above) · no operational control of your
property · no long-term lock-in dressed as opportunity · no pressure toward anything
beyond the listing: **membership** (property-owners are a member class — one member,
one vote, same as a carrier) is there if you ever want a say and a stake, and the
answer "just pay me per drop" is a complete, respected answer forever. If, seasons
from now, your utilization history is strong, there may be a conversation about
contributing the parcel for co-op shares at independent appraisal — **an invitation
you could decline, not a buyout path, and I'm mentioning it now only so nothing later
feels like a bait-and-switch.**
