## What this is

A carrier-owned cooperative runs **drop-and-hook staging** near your facility: trailers
wait at a yard, not on your apron, and arrive when the window opens — because the yard
absorbed the variance your appointment book can't. We're not selling you software and
we're not after your customers. We're offering three things a dock actually feels, and
asking for twenty minutes.

## The three things

**1 — Arrivals that match the book** *(where the corridor runs).* A staged trailer
five minutes away doesn't arrive at 9 a.m. for a 4 p.m. window and doesn't no-show at
4:05. The variance goes to the yard; your doors get the schedule back. Where staging
isn't live yet, we'll say so plainly — see the bracket below.

**2 — The accessorial argument, ended in both directions.** Every touch at your dock —
arrive, depart, window worked — lands as a signed, timestamped event. So: **a
detention line item either carries its attestation or it cannot be invoiced through
this network at all.** Read that as your protection, because it is: the unverifiable
9-a.m.-arrival claim you dread doesn't get softer here — it gets *unrepresentable*.
The quarterly invoice war becomes a document comparison, and the documents were signed
at the moment, not reconstructed in the argument.

**3 — Your empty windows, earning** *(optional, entirely yours).* If it suits the
facility, idle dock windows can list as bookable capacity — at a rate schedule **you
declare in advance**, with one counterparty, on terms you can read in a page. And the
booking system structurally cannot overbook you: allocations above your declared
capacity are impossible to even record, not just discouraged.

## The measurement question (asked before you ask it)

Yes — facilities in this network are measured: dwell, appointment integrity, how
carriers are treated. Three things make that different from being graded by vibes:
**it's computed only from those signed events** (never opinions), **it's floored** (no
number publishes until enough independent visits exist — no single-visit gotchas), and
**it's symmetric**: you see exactly the measurements the network sees, which means a
well-run dock like yours finally *shows up as one* — in the pricing, where it counts.
Improvement is in your hands because the basis is in your view.

## What we're asking today

Twenty minutes and a walkthrough: doors, hours, scheduling quirks, constraints — the
facility facts. Plus your war stories: the no-shows, the detention fights, where
paperwork sticks. Those interviews are how this network decides what to build; your
answers carry weight, and there's an early-member path if you ever want more than a
listing. Window listing afterward is optional, separate, and needs whoever signs for
the facility — **[your read on who that is: ____]**.

## What we will never do

No operational control of your dock — your schedule stays yours · **your customer
relationships are untouchable**: relationship data is sovereign in this system's
constitution and cannot be granted, scraped, or inferred into the commons · no
exclusivity — list windows and keep every other arrangement you have · no scores from
anecdotes (see above) · no numbers promised on paper — where they exist, they're shown
live from the record, because they're real and because they're not ours to print.
