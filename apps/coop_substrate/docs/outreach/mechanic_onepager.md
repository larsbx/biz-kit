## The inversion

You know the arrangement: a fleet owns your schedule, your van, your parts margin —
and none of it owes you anything past the hour. The service co-op is that arrangement
turned right-side up: **the truck you roll belongs to the worker co-op you're a member
of** — you and the other mechanics own the fleet, the tooling, and the books,
together. You share in what it earns and vote on how it runs: one member, one vote,
wrench-turner and senior-most alike.

## What changes on an actual Tuesday

- **Dispatch offers arrive; acceptance is yours.** Nobody owns your yes. And a job you
  *have* accepted is never preempted — no dispatcher yanks you off a call for a
  "better" one.
- **The rate schedule is published in advance — nobody haggles you at 2 a.m. on a
  shoulder.** The card is set by the co-op's own governance (that's you, voting);
  an invoice off the card is not discouraged, it's *unrepresentable* — the system
  cannot write one. The haggle isn't against the rules; it has nowhere to exist.
- **Your hours are never the freebie.** The freight network's members pay dues for
  *access* and then pay **per call, at the member rate, every time** — dues never
  subsidize calls, by rule. A co-op that gave your work away as a membership perk
  would be stealing from its own workers; this one structurally can't.

## Where the work comes from

A built-in base: the freight co-op's member carriers and, in time, its yards — fleets
that need exactly what you sell, routed to the service co-op at the card. Market work
fills the gaps at market rates, capacity-subordinated so member calls and your accepted
jobs come first. **[Formation stage: that base is being built now — see the bracket
below.]** No volume promises on this page or any page; demand shows in the record, and
the record is what you'd see.

## The honest paperwork (the part this industry poisoned)

You've seen 1099 games dressed as opportunity. Here, **what you are — the rights, the
obligations, the tax shape of worker-membership — is defined in the co-op's chartered
documents, reviewed by counsel per state, and you read them before you sign them.**
Not improvised, not retrofitted after a dispute. If those documents don't exist yet
for your state, the honest answer is "not yet," and you'll hear it in those words.

## Sovereignty, both directions

The workforce line is a wall in the constitution: the carriers' co-op never touches
the service co-op's workers, and no carrier's drivers get conscripted into service
work — or the reverse. Peer co-ops, sovereign each way. The freight side is a
*customer* of the service side, at the card, forever.

## Where this stands *(pick the true one — delete the other)*

- **[Formation]** The service co-op is being chartered now — which means the founding
  worker-members draft its rules: the card's first version, the fleet-capital shape
  (buy-in vs earned-in vs loan — genuinely open, and the founders decide it), the
  works. You wouldn't be joining a thing; you'd be building the thing you join.
- **[Operating]** The service co-op runs today. The card, the charter, and the books
  are readable before you commit — ask and they're in front of you.

## The ask

An hour of your experience: how mobile work actually finds you, what the 2 a.m. call
really costs, what a fleet you part-owned would have to do differently. Brutal honesty
welcome — it's design input here, and it comes with early-member standing. If the
inversion reads right after that, the next step is the chartered paperwork, read slow.
