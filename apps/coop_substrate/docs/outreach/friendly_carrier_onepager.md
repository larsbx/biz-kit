## What this is

A group of us are building a **carrier-owned cooperative**: the back-office software,
the brokerage, and eventually the yards — owned by the members who use them, one member
one vote, with every captured middleman margin resolving to member-visible statements.
The rules aren't a pitch; they're written down as a constitution the software enforces:
no interest-bearing instruments, no speculation, nobody's drivers or customers ever
touched, nothing mandatory — bypass is always allowed.

**Where it stands, plainly:** the ledger, the rulebook, and the evidence pipeline are
built and working. No freight moves through it yet. That's deliberate — the co-op's own
rules forbid building the dispatch tool from assumptions. It gets built from what
working carriers say, or not at all.

## What we're asking of you

**About an hour**, on a call or over coffee, about how dispatch actually works at your
operation: how tenders really arrive, what you check before saying yes, who assigns the
driver, where the paperwork sticks, and the question everything hangs on — **what would
you let software accept on your behalf, and where exactly does your trust stop?**

Your answers become the specification. Five to eight carriers' answers, cross-checked
against each other, gate whether anything gets built at all.

## What you get

- **First say.** The interview cohort becomes the founding cohort if you want in:
  charter-drafting seats, and the operating constants set by the people who'll live
  under them. Founding standing is recorded permanently. One member, one vote — founding
  buys standing, never extra votes.
- **The tool shaped around your operation** — if it gets built, the first thing it runs
  on is a founding carrier's real books, where enabled and once verification gates pass.
- **Early-member standing** for the hour itself (an honorarium instead where our counsel
  has cleared it in your state).

## What we will never do — these are structural, not policy

- **No recruiting commissions, ever.** Nobody here is paid for bringing names. An
  invitation creates bounded responsibility for the inviter, not a bounty. The software
  cannot represent a recruitment payment.
- **No touching your authority, your drivers, your customers.** Carrier sovereignty is
  the constitution's second article. The co-op never dispatches your trucks and never
  sees your customer list unless you choose to share a specific thing, in writing.
- **No promises of rates, volumes, or savings.** Nothing is live; anyone promising you
  numbers today would be lying. What the system does is make evidence visible, terms
  declared, and exits legible.

## How your words are protected

Before the interview starts, consent is **your signature, cryptographically** — you get
the key, you keep it. You choose exactly what your words may be used for (shaping the
spec; anonymized test data; a follow-up about membership) and each use is checked by the
software, not by anyone's memory. Recording only happens if you separately say yes.
**You can revoke at any time with one signed message**, and everything of yours is
pulled from every count and every document the moment it lands — even if that stalls the
whole project. That's not a courtesy; it's how the system is built, and I'll show you.

## The ask

An hour, your dispatch war stories, and your honest answer about where trust stops.
If what we're building is wrong, the interview is where you get to kill it cheaply.
