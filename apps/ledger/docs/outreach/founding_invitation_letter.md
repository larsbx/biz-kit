# Founding-cohort invitation letter — template

*Sent ONLY after the D slice runs live on the first hub's books (corpus 13 §6: "a demo
that doesn't run isn't a demo" — this letter's precondition is the dispatch kickoff
checklist's 4D box). Recipients: interviewed carriers whose consent includes
`prospect_record`, hub-scored per your private tracker. Still zero numbers — the real
numbers now exist, but they are the HUB's bilateral data: the letter routes proof
through the hub showing their own phone (13 §4), never through quoted figures.
**[HUB]'s agreement to be named and demo is a precondition — ask first.** One letter,
one person; never a mail-merge blast.*

---

Dear **[first name]**,

You're getting this because of the hour you gave me **[when — "back in March, at the
yard"]** — you said I could follow up, and this is the follow-up. It's a different
letter than I could have written then, because of one fact:

**The thing got built, and it's running.** Not a pitch deck — the dispatch back-office
we interviewed you about is live on **[HUB first name]**'s operation right now: his
tenders, his paperwork, his detention evidence, on his books. And it was built *from*
those interviews — your answers about where trust stops are literally in its rules; the
software refused to be built until enough of you had spoken. That was the promise, and
it held.

I'm not going to put his numbers in a letter — they're his. **Ask him. He'll show you
his phone.** That demo, from a man whose word you already trust, is the whole pitch,
and if it doesn't impress you, no letter should.

**What I'm inviting you into** is the founding cohort — the small group that turns this
from one carrier's tool into a member-owned cooperative:

- **A seat drafting the charter.** The operating rules — dues, thresholds, every
  constant this thing runs on — get set by the people who'll live under them. That's
  not a courtesy; the system enforces whatever this cohort writes.
- **One member, one vote — including you, including me, forever.** Money buys more
  participation in the economics, never more say. Founding buys standing and a
  permanent place in the record, never extra votes.
- **A declared, time-boxed founding offer**: a dues waiver for a first period the
  charter will fix, and an invitation allotment — the right to bring people in, which
  here means *taking bounded responsibility for them*, not earning from them.
- **First access as it grows**: the same back-office on your own books, and — when the
  co-op's brokerage goes live, where enabled — your routed freight at open-book margin,
  voluntary, load by load.

**What this is not, structurally**: no recruitment pay of any kind (the software cannot
record one), no share discounts (issuance is at appraised value, for everyone, always),
no revenue guarantees (anyone promising you outcomes is lying, and the rules make the
lie hard to keep). And nothing about your authority, your drivers, or your customers
changes hands — that's the constitution's second article, same as I told you at the
interview.

**The ask**: come to the founding session — **[date, time, place]**. Bring your
skepticism; it's been the most useful thing carriers have given this project. Drafting
the charter will take real hours across a few sittings, and joining afterward is one
signature, yours, only if what got drafted deserves it.

And the standing exit, same as always: reply "no more" and this is the last letter —
that permission you gave me is revocable like everything else was.

**[FOUNDER name]**
**[contact]**

---

*Eligibility & candor footer: membership is gated (conduct standing, sponsorship,
verification) and any benefit described applies once admitted and where the relevant
program is live. The founding offer's levers are declared and time-boxed in the charter
this cohort drafts; they never include recruitment payments, share discounts, or
guarantees. This invitation creates no obligation, and declining it changes nothing
about how you're treated.*
