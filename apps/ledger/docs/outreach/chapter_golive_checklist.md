# Second-chapter go-live checklist — the first mirrored handoff

*The federation's first operational fact: a trailer crossing from one sovereign
chapter's member to another's at a boundary yard, recorded as the inter-chapter
agreement's Article 3 prescribes — **two attestations, two logs, each
referencing the other's hash, no shared machinery anywhere.** Tonight the
mirror discipline stops being a treaty clause and becomes someone's cold hands
at a fence line. Its exam: the pair reconciles — each chapter can verify its
half and point at the other's. Its hardest refusal is the reasonable-sounding
one: **"let's just put both sides on one log for simplicity"** — the simplicity
is the sovereignty leaving. Inherited family rules: real freight, rehearsed
before real, misses-first tonight (bilaterally).*

---

## 0 — Preconditions (both chapters, each on its own log)

- [ ] The inter-chapter agreement signed per its template — mirror
      declarations on BOTH logs, one shared artifact hash; read both back
- [ ] `corridor_activation_checklist.md` closed including §6 (treaty lock):
      first checkpoints exchanged, mutual witness running
- [ ] The boundary yard's own night one done (`yard_golive_checklist.md`,
      run by whichever chapter holds the listing) — the fence line is proven
      ground before it hosts a treaty event
- [ ] **The mirror procedure rehearsed BILATERALLY on both test logs**,
      including the ordering below — both signers have done the dance once
      with nothing at stake
- [ ] The two members doing the handoff briefed on the discipline in plain
      words: sign your side, on your chapter's log, share your event hash,
      reference theirs — and both hold the same paper condition report
      either way

## 1 — The ordering (scripted, because "who appends first" is not obvious at a fence)

- [ ] Agreed in advance, per the rehearsal: the RELEASING side appends first
      (its attestation carries the condition report and its signature), then
      shares its event hash at the fence — by any channel both trust; the
      hash is public-to-members anyway
- [ ] The RECEIVING side appends second, its own attestation referencing the
      releasing event's hash — the mirror closed
- [ ] The releasing side records the counterpart hash in a follow-up
      reference per the rehearsed pattern **[or, where the v0 pattern
      carries only one direction: the one-way reference is the discipline,
      stated honestly — the pair still reconciles by comparing the two
      attestations' shared condition facts]**
- [ ] Total elapsed target from hook to closed mirror: minutes, not days —
      a mirror that closes next week is a dispute surface all week

## 2 — The handoff (the physical half)

- [ ] The one-pattern at the fence: condition attested at the moment, both
      parties' signatures on the hook, photos per the pattern — the paper
      report exists even if every phone at the fence is dead (the physical
      record never waits for the digital one)
- [ ] Both members keep their copies on the spot — each side's protection in
      each side's hands
- [ ] Nothing crosses but the trailer and the report: no keys, no log access,
      no operator of one chapter touching the other's machine — sovereignty
      is physical tonight

## 3 — The verification (the exam)

- [ ] Same day: each chapter verifies its own half (`Log.verify_chains` →
      :ok, the attestation where it belongs) and confirms the cross-reference
      resolves — the counterpart hash names a real event the other chapter
      can produce
- [ ] The reconciliation demonstrated once, in the open: the two attestations
      compared — same trailer, same condition facts, adjacent signatures —
      "disputes reduce to comparing these two documents" shown true on a
      handoff nobody is disputing, so the first disputed one has a precedent
- [ ] Checkpoints emitted BOTH ends and exchanged — Article 6's cadence gets
      a mid-cycle beat tonight; the first treaty event deserves a sealed head
      on both sides

## 4 — Same-day after (bilaterally symmetric)

- [ ] Each chapter debriefs ITS OWN member — no joint retro, no cross-chapter
      relay of what either said; network conduct issues go to each chapter's
      own standing review
- [ ] Misses-first, mirror-one edition, written tonight in EACH chapter's own
      terms: the hash that took three tries to share, the append that lagged,
      the briefing gap — and exchanged as aggregates between governances if
      both choose (counts, not transcripts)
- [ ] The weekly status gains a corridor-boundary line each side: handoffs 1,
      mirrors closed 1/1, mirror-close time ____, disputes 0
- [ ] The 09 §1 question fed with its first real data: whatever tonight's
      friction was, it goes on the joint agenda as evidence for (or against)
      federation-scoped events — the open question stays open, but now it has
      a data point

## 5 — What does NOT happen tonight (refusals, verified)

- [ ] **No shared log, no merged stream, no "both sides on one machine for
      simplicity"** — the mirror's friction is the price of two sovereignties,
      and it's paid, not engineered away ad hoc; if the friction argues for
      federation-scoped events, that argument goes through 09 §1 and both
      governances, never through a fence-line improvisation
- [ ] No one holds the other chapter's keys, credentials, or log access —
      not even "just for tonight," not even the founder
- [ ] No invented event types: the rehearsed v0 pattern (attestation +
      hash reference via the existing machinery) is what runs; a dedicated
      cross-chapter event type is a governance decision with a signature,
      not a launch-night patch
- [ ] No cross-chapter debrief relay — two bilateral stories, two aggregates,
      zero transcripts crossing
- [ ] No announcement beyond the folds, in EITHER chapter: one handoff, one
      closed mirror, zero disputes — counts, not adjectives, on two logs
