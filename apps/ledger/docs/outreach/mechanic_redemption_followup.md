# Worker-member redemption follow-up — template (service co-op internal capital)

*Unlike the facility-equity case (one letter, one machinery — see the property
redemption follow-up's holder-tenant note), worker-co-op equity IS a distinct
instrument and earns its own letter: an **internal capital account** — accrued from
her own labor's patronage, paid out on a schedule when she leaves — running on the
substrate's redemption machinery (schedules, annual caps, FIFO consumption, sinking
fund, death-to-estate), under worker-classification counsel (Tier 3.4), not
securities law. **The sender is the service co-op's own governance** — this template
is a gift to its founders; the freight co-op never writes letters about a peer
co-op's internal accounts, by constitution. Sent to the departing or retiring
worker-member when her schedule opens. Zero projections, series rules throughout.*

---

**Subject:** Your capital account — the full accounting, and the schedule ahead
*(alt: "What your years built: the arithmetic, and how it pays")*

Dear **[first name]**,

With your **[retirement/departure]** now recorded, your capital account has moved to
redemption — and this letter is the full accounting you were promised the day you
signed the worker-membership documents. Every number below recomputes from the
record; nothing is a summary you have to trust.

**What your years built:** over [period], your work credited **[amount]** to your
account — every credit on the record with the rule that computed it, from the
patronage weights this co-op's members (you included) voted. The itemized history is
attached/viewable; it is your labor, in ledger form.

**How it pays:** your schedule runs **[Y] years at up to [cap] per year**, consuming
your oldest credits first — the standard terms this co-op declared before anyone's
schedule opened, applied to you exactly as to everyone. **[First payment: date,
amount, method.]** Each payment lands on the record as it happens; if a date ever
slips, you hear it from us before you notice it.

**Why a schedule and not a check — said honestly:** your account pays over years
because the trucks and tools you helped buy keep working for the members still
turning wrenches. A co-op that cashed out every exit instantly would eat its own
fleet — the sinking fund exists so departures are honored *without* being paid by
the people staying. Your patience is part of what you built; the schedule is its
declared, capped, recorded shape — never a renegotiation.

**What your exit does NOT touch:** your standing here (you leave in good standing
and the record says so) · the door back (rejoining is a fresh membership, and your
history is a recommendation, not a complication) · and — said plainly because it
matters and nobody likes saying it — **if you die mid-schedule, the balance routes
to your estate**; that provision is in the machinery, not in anyone's goodwill.

**What ends with membership, honestly:** your vote. One member one vote was about
working membership; the account is yours until paid, the governance belongs to the
people currently on the trucks. You knew this from the documents; it deserves saying
out loud anyway.

**What didn't go right, from us first:**

- **[The honest list — the accounting question that took a week, the first-payment
  processing delay. A first-ever redemption WILL have friction; name it. If truly
  nothing: the luck-not-design humility, first-cycle edition.]**

**No ask.** This letter closes an account the right way; it sells nothing. Thank you
for the calls at 2 a.m. and everything they built — some of which, per the above,
is still yours and arriving on schedule.

With respect,

**[SERVICE CO-OP GOVERNANCE — names/roles]**
**[contact]**

## Usage notes (internal to the SERVICE CO-OP's governance — do not send)

- **This letter is yours, not the federation's** — the freight co-op provided the
  template and the machinery; the accounting, the signatures, and the relationship
  are this worker co-op's alone (peer sovereignty, both directions).
- **Verify the account arithmetic against the log before drafting** — the bed-count
  discipline: her total is the number she can recompute herself; being wrong in
  this letter is being wrong about someone's years.
- **The schedule terms must be the STANDARD ones** declared before any schedule
  opened — a departing member offered special terms (better or worse) breaks the
  applied-exactly-as-to-everyone sentence and the equal-treatment it stands on.
- **FIFO explained plainly** — "oldest credits first" in words, because the
  machinery's fairness is invisible if unexplained.
- **Tax documents per counsel (Tier 3.4)** accompany the first payment — the
  worker-classification review defined this payout's shape; its paperwork is part
  of the payment, not a follow-up.
- **The estate paragraph stays in** — deleting it for comfort deletes the one
  promise whose test nobody schedules.
- **Payment slips announced first** — series standard, applied to the years this
  schedule runs, not just the letter's week.
- **The precedent belongs to everyone**: the first redemption paying correctly is
  every worker-member's information — share the AGGREGATE fact at the next
  governance meeting (a schedule opened, payments on time, terms standard); her
  individual figures stay hers (bilateral, always).
- **No re-pitch, no "consider staying," no counter-offer** in the letter — the
  decision was made; the letter honors it. Retention conversations, if any, happened
  before the lifecycle event, never after.
